# Workshop Activities: Canvas Notes

Source of truth: [Workshop Activities](https://capstone.alexulbrich.com/assignments/workshop-activities/).

## Point values

Unlike the other rubrics in this directory, these do **not** total 100. Each workshop item is worth **10 points**, so the assignment's point total varies with how many items the term has:

| Term | Items | Canvas assignment points | Share of term grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fall | 6 | 60 | 2% |
| Winter | 1 | 10 | 1% |
| Spring | 1 | 10 | 1% |

The percentage comes from the assignment-group weight, not from the rubric total. Ten points per item keeps every item an equal integer share, which matters because these are pass/fail and partial credit within an item does not exist.

## Files

- `workshop-activities-fall-rubric-details.tsv`: 6 rows, use in CS 461.
- `workshop-activities-term-rubric-details.tsv`: 1 row, use in CS 462 and CS 463. Edit the Pass description to name that term's instrument (Team Dysfunctions Assessment in winter, Team Health Assessment in spring).

## Grading

Pass or Fail per item, nothing in between. Pass means something real was submitted that is recognizably the activity. Fail means nothing was submitted, or the submission is not this activity (a blank file, an unrelated document).

**Do not grade quality.** There is no rubric band for a better story map or a more thorough safety-net audit. A grader spends seconds per item. If you find yourself forming an opinion about how good the output is, you are grading the wrong thing: the quality signal lands later, on the criteria these activities feed.

## Late work

A workshop item submitted any time before the end of the term still earns its points. The standard late policy does not apply here, because doing the activity late costs the student the benefit rather than the mark, and chasing timestamps on a 10-point pass/fail item is not worth TA time.

## Why these are graded at all

Each item feeds several rubric criteria in later assignments, and every year some teams skip the cheap preparation and pay for it in week 8. The points are small enough not to be worth optimizing and large enough that skipping them is a visible choice. A skipped workshop is also a useful signal: paired with a low score on the criterion it feeds, it is a legitimate trigger for the individual contribution modifier review.
