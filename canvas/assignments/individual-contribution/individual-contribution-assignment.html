Individual (no submission; graded from sprint evidence)

Purpose

  • Half of every Sprint Note's points are individual. This assignment is where that individual half lives in Canvas.
  • It protects students who contribute: team scores are not dragged down by a teammate who disengaged, and contributors are not penalized for someone else's silence.
  • It makes accountability structural, not testimonial: the evidence is your repository record, not what teammates say about you.

What You Must Produce

Nothing. There is no submission and nothing to prepare for this assignment. It defaults to full credit for every student.

Your evidence stream already exists if you are doing the work:

  • You own at least one reviewed pull request per sprint, as agreed in your Team Charter. "Owns" means you authored or drove it, responded to review, and merged it.
  • Your team's Sprint Notes carry a contribution line for you each sprint (PR titles or IDs; NDA teams list IDs only).
  • The Defense spot-verifies ownership claims live.

How It Is Graded

Graders deduct from your score only when a sprint's evidence (contribution lines, repo record, review activity) shows no meaningful contribution during that sprint. Each sprint of the term carries an equal share of the points and is scored full, half, or zero, with an evidence note for any deduction. See the rubric page for the deduction logic.

  • Deductions follow the corroboration process on the partner evaluation page: graders check the repo record and review activity before deducting, and you can respond with evidence.
  • Missing your PR-per-sprint norm once, with an explanation in the sprint note, is normal life and costs nothing. A pattern of missed sprints with no explanation is what this assignment exists for.
  • Reviewed pull requests are the default evidence, but any verifiable contribution counts if your team's charter and sprint note make it traceable (e.g., substantial reviews, documentation, test or infrastructure work).

For the full architecture, see the Assignments Overview in the handbook.

Do not fabricate evidence. Claiming credit for work you did not produce is academic misconduct; all PRs, reviews, and links must be real.
