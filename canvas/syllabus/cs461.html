Assignments and Grading

This is a software engineering project for your project partner, and you deliver what you agree with them. That agreement can move, so check in often.

We'll give you feedback along the way. The focus is less on whether you can make it work and more on whether your team can deliver consistently, though your personal ambition sets the ceiling.

Check the handbook for details. Canvas is the source of truth and submissions happen here.

Grade Components

Each term, your grade is composed of four equal components.

Component Weight Details (Fall)
Project Partner Interactions 25% How your team works with your project partner, scored by them: midterm pulse survey 5% (sent week 5, closes end of week 6) + final survey 20% (sent week 9, closes end of week 10). Where a team has no project partner, a mentor, an instructor, or a TA stands in and scores the same facets.
Peer Evaluation 25% Midterm survey 5% (sent week 5, closes end of week 6) + final survey 20% (sent week 9, closes end of week 10)
Individual Evidence 25% RFC 15% (draft week 4, peer review week 5, revision and decision week 8) + Defense 8% (week 7 or 9, a session of its own) + Resume and Intent 2% (week 2; resume meeting with an instructor in weeks 3 to 5)
Team Deliverables 25% Term Startup 1% (week 2, complete/incomplete), Team Charter 3% (week 2), Workshop Activities 2% (5 items, complete/incomplete, weeks 2, 3, 7, 8), Sprint Notes 4 × 2% (weeks 4, 6, 8, 10), Repo Checkpoints 2 × 4% (weeks 5, 10), Term Retrospective 3% (week 9)

Team deliverables carry an individual contribution modifier: half of each sprint's weight is an Individual Contribution entry of its own, which defaults to full credit and is reduced when the sprint's evidence falls short of the contribution floor; a sprint with no contribution scores zero.

Demo Day: your team presents once during the year, at one of five all-hands sessions. Presenting earns 1% extra credit in the term you present, added on top of the four components.

Late Assignments

Work up to 48 hours late loses one rubric band on each criterion, and beyond 48 hours it scores zero unless you contacted the instructors before the deadline with a documented reason. Sprint notes are one exception: they are evidence of the sprint that just happened, so they are not accepted late. Workshops earn credit only in class or by the end of the week the class happened. Planned conflicts need at least 48 hours' notice, and documented emergencies always have a path; the full policy, including defense and demo makeup, is the Late Work, Absence, and Makeup section of the handbook. Contact cs-46X-help@oregonstate.edu as soon as you can if you are dealing with an emergency.

Attendance Policy

Attendance at mandatory class segments is expected. More importantly, you are expected to show up reliably for your team: sprint meetings, check-ins, and any commitments made to your project partner. Unreliability within a team is a professional issue and will factor into peer and partner evaluations.

AI Policy

In this course, you are encouraged to experiment with AI and GenAI tools. You should not enter creative works, sensitive data, or personally identifiable information into GenAI tools. Do not fabricate evidence. Check the validity and correctness of your GenAI output. You are fully responsible for what you code and submit.

Delegate deliberately, and build the checks that make it reasonable. Major deliverables ask three things: what you handed to AI tools, what checks made that reasonable (tests, CI, review gates, staging, a rollback path), and where you stopped to validate the result yourself. Validate carefully where a decision is hard to reverse: architecture, database schema, authentication, deployment, data migrations, licensing. Delegate freely where your checks catch mistakes cheaply.

What is graded is the fit between how far you delegated and how strong your checks are, never how sophisticated your tooling is. See the AI policy and the AI practice activities in the handbook.

Sick Policy

Students should not attend class or any public gatherings while ill with COVID-19, influenza, or any other contagion. Students with symptoms will be asked to leave the classroom or instructor’s office and return home for recovery. If you have COVID-19, influenza, or another virus or contagion, the illness and self-isolation period may be two weeks or longer. Most students should be able to complete a successful term despite such an absence. Students who are absent because of illness will be provided with a reasonable opportunity to make up missed work. Ordinarily, it is inappropriate to substitute for the missed assignment the weighting of a term's work that does not include the missed assignment or exam. Completion of all assignments and exams assures the greatest chance for students to develop heightened understanding and content mastery that is unavailable through the weighting process. The opportunity to complete all assignments and exams is intended to enable students to make responsible situational decisions, including the decision to avoid spreading a contagion to other students, staff, and faculty, without in any way endangering their academic opportunities and work. Students with a contagion do not need to provide a physician's certification of illness. Ill students should inform the instruction team via email or through Canvas (but not through personal contact) as soon as possible that they will be absent because of an illness. The instruction team will work with them to arrange to make up missed assignments or exams. The above considerations also apply if students are caring for someone that is ill. The Office of the Dean of Students can assist students if they are navigating a range of extenuating life circumstances including but not limited to prolonged illness, hospitalization, and financial concerns. They can be reached Monday through Friday from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM by calling 541-737-8748 or emailing support.odos@oregonstate.edu. If illness prevents you from meeting your team commitments, notify your team as early as possible so work can be redistributed; don’t wait until a deadline has passed.

The Student Conduct Code establishes community standards and procedures necessary to maintain and protect an environment conducive to learning, in keeping with the educational objectives of Oregon State University. This code is based on the assumption that all persons must treat one another with dignity and respect in order for scholarship to thrive. Students are expected to abide by OSU’s Code of Student Conduct, which they are encouraged to read at https://beav.es/codeofconduct.

Professionalism in this course is as important as in the workplace and includes such traits as preparation, productivity, and promptness. Cell phones, email, texting, and all other potential distractions will not be tolerated during regularly scheduled class meetings or during office hours.

Examples of Academic Misconduct

Please note the following examples of what is considered inappropriate in this course.

  • Misrepresenting your individual contributions to a team deliverable, sprint, or report.
  • Claiming credit for code, documentation, or design work you did not produce.
  • Fabricating or misrepresenting project progress, meeting notes, or project partner interactions in reports or demos.
  • Submitting written work (reflections, reports) generated entirely by AI as your own original writing.
  • Sharing confidential project partner information, code, or data outside of the course without authorization.
  • Incorporating code under a license (e.g., GPL, AGPL) incompatible with your project partner’s intended use, without disclosing this to your team and project partner.
  • Referencing or providing course materials to any individual or 3rd party company (e.g., Chegg, Course Hero).

Student Evaluation of Courses

During Fall, Winter, and Spring term the online Student Learning Experience surveys open to students the Wednesday of week 9 and close the Sunday before Finals Week. Students will receive notification, instructions and the link through their ONID email. They may also log into the system via MyOregonState or directly at beav.es/Student-Learning-Survey. Survey results are extremely important and are used to help improve courses and the learning experience of future students. Responses are anonymous (unless a student chooses to “sign” their comments, agreeing to relinquish anonymity of written comments) and are not available to instructors until after grades have been posted. The results of scaled questions and signed comments go to both the instructor and their unit head/supervisor. Anonymous (unsigned) comments go to the instructor only.
