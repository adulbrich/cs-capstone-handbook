This section is a **practice library**: a collection of well-crafted exercises you can run with your team or on your own. They exist to help you execute your project well and to prepare you for the [graded assignments](/assignments/introduction/), which say what's due and when.

The activities are organized by theme:

- [AI Practice](/activities/ai/): hard-to-reverse decisions, safety nets, agent-driven testing, acceptance judgment.
- [Career Preparation](/activities/career/): resumes, portfolios, interviews, networking.
- [Communication](/activities/communication/): presenting, writing, accessibility.
- [Conflict Resolution](/activities/conflict/): conflict styles, performance conversations, escalation paths. The explanation lives in the [conflict guide](/guides/conflict/).
- [Creative](/activities/creative/): ideation, brainstorming, design sprints.
- [Design](/activities/design/): architecture, prototypes, technology evaluation, security.
- [Planning](/activities/planning/): metrics, prioritization, risk, scheduling.
- [Reflective](/activities/reflective/): reading, watching, journaling, external feedback.
- [Requirements](/activities/requirements/): canvases, user stories, research questions.
- [Teamwork](/activities/teamwork/): stand-ups, team health, dysfunction assessments.
- [User](/activities/user/): finding real users, interviews, personas, UX maturity.

## Three Tiers

Activities carry a badge telling you how much attention each deserves.

<Badge text="Workshop" variant="caution" class="mb-6"/> Nine activities everyone does: five in fall, three in winter, one in spring. These run as workshops in class and are graded [complete/incomplete](/assignments/workshop-activities/); the weights and the item list live on that page, and the [schedule](/introduction/schedule/) has the week each one runs. There's no quality bar: submit the output and you get the credit.

Every workshop runs the exercise by hand. A second run with an AI agent is optional, and it shows where delegation helps and where it misleads. [Resume Building](/activities/career/#resume-building) also runs as a session in spring, alongside the [Career and Individual Retrospective](/assignments/career-retrospective/); it isn't a workshop item.

<Badge text="Recommended" variant="tip" class="mb-6"/> The cheapest route to a specific rubric criterion. Every graded assignment page names the recommended activities that prepare it, and says which criterion each one serves. If you're staring at a rubric wondering where to start, start there. Repeated assignments (sprint notes, repo checkpoints) name different activities for different instances, so you're never asked to redo the same exercise.

Everything else is the **library**. More than a hundred further exercises, kept because they're good and because your project may need exactly one of them. Nobody expects you to do most of them. Each assignment page also names one or two activity **categories** to browse when the specific recommendations run out.

## Not Every Project Needs the Same Preparation

A team contributing patches upstream, a team running experiments with a professor, a team building software for an external partner, and a team inventing a product don't need the same preparation. Most assignment pages carry an **Examples** table naming the activities a project like yours usually needs, alongside the ones everybody does. Take the closest row if none of them is quite your project; the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) works four paths through in full.

If your project is research or R&D, the ones worth knowing about early are [Define Your Research Questions](/activities/requirements/#define-your-research-questions), [Map Your External Approvals](/activities/planning/#map-your-external-approvals) and [Complete an IRB Application](/activities/user/#complete-an-irb-application) (both in fall week 1, because neither clock runs faster for starting late), [Identify Success Metrics](/activities/planning/#identify-success-metrics), [Reproduce Your Baseline](/activities/design/#reproduce-your-baseline), [Make Your Artifact Reproducible](/activities/design/#make-your-artifact-reproducible), and [Keep an Experiment Log](/activities/reflective/#keep-an-experiment-log).

## How to Use This Library

Work from the rubric rather than the whole list: read the rubric for what's due next, do the recommended activities it names, and leave the rest until you need them. Every activity ends with what a good output looks like, and most state the effort involved, so you can judge the investment before starting.

Do the nine workshop activities regardless, because they're the ones that pay off more than once.

You're encouraged to email the instruction team for early feedback on your progress.
