This section is a **practice library**: a collection of well-crafted exercises you can run with your team or on your own. They exist to help you execute your project well and to prepare you for the [graded assignments](/assignments/introduction/), which say what's due and when.

The activities are organized by the kind of work:

- [Team and Workflow](/activities/team-and-workflow/): team formation, working and conflict styles, the board, stand-ups, team health.
- [Planning and Risk](/activities/planning-and-risk/): approvals, stakeholders, risks, dependencies.
- [Requirements](/activities/requirements/): canvases, hypotheses, personas, user stories, prioritization, success metrics.
- [Working with Users](/activities/working-with-users/): interview questions, finding and engaging users, UX maturity.
- [Ideation](/activities/ideation/): brainstorming, SCAMPER, design sprints.
- [Research Methods](/activities/research-methods/): literature reviews, research questions, data, baselines, experiment logs.
- [Technical Design](/activities/technical-design/): technology choices, architecture, schemas, security, design review.
- [Interaction Design and Accessibility](/activities/interaction-design/): prototypes, UI guidelines, game design, accessibility.
- [Testing, Release, and Handoff](/activities/testing-release-and-handoff/): test plans, safety nets, deployment, releases, maintenance, documentation.
- [Presenting](/activities/presenting/): talks, stage fright, external feedback.
- [Learning and Reflection](/activities/learning-and-reflection/): learning journals, reading, watching, listening.
- [Career](/activities/career/): resumes, portfolios, interviews, networking.

## Three Tiers

Workshop and recommended activities open with a line of badges built from the assignment pages, so it always agrees with them. The line tells you how much attention the activity deserves and which graded work it serves. A library activity carries one only when it has a Whole team or effort badge.

<Badge text="Workshop 1, Fall" variant="caution"/> Nine activities everyone does: five in fall, three in winter, one in spring. These run as workshops in class and are graded [complete/incomplete](/assignments/workshop-activities/); the badge links the workshop's entry on that page, which holds the weights and the item list, and the [schedule](/introduction/schedule/) has the week each one runs. There's no quality bar: submit the output and you get the credit.

Every workshop runs the exercise by hand. A second run with an AI agent is optional, and it shows where delegation helps and where it misleads. [Resume Building](/activities/career/#resume-building) also runs as a session in spring, alongside the [Career and Individual Retrospective](/assignments/career-retrospective/); it isn't a workshop item.

Prepares: <Badge text="Team Charter" variant="tip"/> A recommended activity: the cheapest route to a specific rubric criterion. Each badge after **Prepares** names an assignment whose page recommends the activity, and links the section of that page saying which criterion it serves. If you're staring at a rubric wondering where to start, start there. Repeated assignments (sprint notes, repo checkpoints) name different activities for different instances, so you're never asked to redo the same exercise. Each assignment page also names the activity pages to browse when its recommendations run out.

Two more badges appear where they apply: **Whole team** on an activity that doesn't work unless everyone is there, and the effort, from 15 minutes to ongoing, where it's known.

Everything else is the **library**: exercises kept because they're good and because your project may need exactly one of them. Nobody expects you to do most of them.

Beyond the nine workshops, more than a hundred activities, recommended and library alike, are optional.

## Not Every Project Needs the Same Preparation

A team contributing patches upstream, a team running experiments with a professor, a team building software for an external partner, and a team inventing a product don't need the same preparation. Several assignment pages carry an **Examples** table naming the activities a project like yours usually needs, alongside the ones everybody does. Take the closest row if none of them is quite your project; the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) works four paths through in full.

If your project is research or R&D, the ones worth knowing about early are [Define Your Research Questions](/activities/research-methods/#define-your-research-questions), [Map Your External Approvals](/activities/planning-and-risk/#map-your-external-approvals) and [Complete an IRB Application](/activities/planning-and-risk/#complete-an-irb-application) (both in fall week 1, because neither clock runs faster for starting late), [Identify Success Metrics](/activities/requirements/#identify-success-metrics), [Reproduce Your Baseline](/activities/research-methods/#reproduce-your-baseline), [Make Your Artifact Reproducible](/activities/research-methods/#make-your-artifact-reproducible), and [Keep an Experiment Log](/activities/research-methods/#keep-an-experiment-log).

## How to Use This Library

Work from the rubric rather than the whole list: read the rubric for what's due next, do the recommended activities it names, and leave the rest until you need them. Every activity ends with what a good output looks like, and those with an effort badge let you judge the investment before starting.

Do the nine workshop activities regardless, because they're the ones that pay off more than once.

You're encouraged to email the instruction team for early feedback on your progress.
