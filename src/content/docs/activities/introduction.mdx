This section is a **practice library**: a collection of well-crafted exercises you can run with your team or on your own. They exist to help you execute your project well and to prepare you for the [graded assignments](/assignments/introduction/), which are the source of truth for what is due and when.

The activities are organized by theme:

- [AI Practice](/activities/ai/): one-way doors, safety nets, agent-driven testing, acceptance judgment.
- [Career Preparation](/activities/career/): resumes, portfolios, interviews, networking.
- [Communication](/activities/communication/): presenting, writing, accessibility.
- [Conflict Resolution](/activities/conflict/): difficult behaviors, performance conversations, conflict styles.
- [Creative](/activities/creative/): ideation, brainstorming, design sprints.
- [Design](/activities/design/): architecture, prototypes, technology evaluation, security.
- [Planning](/activities/planning/): metrics, prioritization, risk, scheduling.
- [Reflective](/activities/reflective/): reading, watching, journaling, external feedback.
- [Requirements](/activities/requirements/): canvases, user stories, research questions.
- [Teamwork](/activities/teamwork/): stand-ups, team health, dysfunction assessments.
- [User](/activities/user/): finding real users, interviews, personas, UX maturity.

## Three Tiers

Activities carry a badge telling you how much attention each deserves.

<Badge text="Workshop" variant="caution" class="mb-6"/> **Six activities everyone does.** These run as workshops in class and are graded [complete/incomplete](/assignments/workshop-activities/): 2% of the fall grade across six items, 1% in winter and 1% in spring. There is no quality bar and no rubric. Submit the output and you get the points.

| Workshop activity | When | What it makes easier |
|---|---|---|
| [Set Up Your Repository's Skills](/activities/ai/#set-up-your-repositorys-skills) | Fall, weeks 1-2 | Your [Team Charter](/assignments/team-charter/) AI context file and confidentiality one-pager |
| [User Story Mapping](/activities/requirements/#user-story-mapping) | Fall, weeks 2-3 | `docs/requirements.md` for every [repo checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/), and the winter [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) |
| [Describe your Architecture](/activities/design/#describe-your-architecture) | Fall, weeks 3-4 | `docs/design.md`, your [RFC](/assignments/rfc/), and the [defense](/assignments/defense/) technical-depth question |
| [Map Your One-Way Doors](/activities/ai/#map-your-one-way-doors) | Fall, weeks 3-4 | Choosing an RFC topic at all, since the RFC must now be a decision that is hard to reverse |
| [Audit Your Safety Net](/activities/ai/#audit-your-safety-net) | Fall, weeks 4-5 | Four criteria across the checkpoints, the RFC, and the defense. The highest-leverage hour in this library |
| A teamwork assessment, rotating | Once per term | [Charter](/assignments/team-charter/) conflict and inclusion, and the defense role question |

The rotating teamwork slot is [Team Formation Strategies](/activities/teamwork/#team-formation-strategies-assessment) in fall, [Team Dysfunctions](/activities/teamwork/#team-dysfunctions-assessment) in winter, and [Team Health](/activities/teamwork/#team-health-assessment) in spring, so it tells you something new each time.

<Badge text="Recommended" variant="tip" class="mb-6"/> **The cheapest route to a specific rubric criterion.** Every graded assignment page names the recommended activities that prepare it, and says which criterion each one serves. If you are staring at a rubric wondering where to start, start there. Repeated assignments (sprint notes, repo checkpoints) name different activities for different instances, so you are never asked to redo the same exercise.

**Everything else is the library.** Sixty more exercises, kept because they are good and because your project may need exactly one of them. Nobody expects you to do most of them. Each assignment page also names one or two activity **categories** worth browsing when the specific recommendations run out.

## Your Project Category Changes the Answer

A team contributing patches upstream, a team running experiments with a professor, a team building software for an external partner, and a team inventing a product do not need the same preparation, and pretending otherwise wastes everyone's time. Most assignment pages carry a **By project category** table naming the activities your [category](/practicalities/categories/) specifically needs, alongside the ones everybody does.

If your project is research or R&D, the ones worth knowing about early are [Define Your Research Questions](/activities/requirements/#define-your-research-questions), [Start the Approval Clocks](/activities/planning/#start-the-approval-clocks) and [Complete an IRB Application](/activities/user/#complete-an-irb-application) (both in fall week 1, because neither clock runs faster for starting late), [Choose Your Evaluation Metric Before You Run Anything](/activities/planning/#choose-your-evaluation-metric-before-you-run-anything), [Reproduce Your Baseline](/activities/design/#reproduce-your-baseline), [Make Your Artifact Reproducible](/activities/design/#make-your-artifact-reproducible), and [Keep an Experiment Log](/activities/reflective/#keep-an-experiment-log).

## How to Use This Library

**Follow the rubric, not the whole list.** The fastest path: read the rubric for what is due next, do the recommended activities it names, and ignore the rest until you need them. Every activity ends with what a good output looks like, and most state the effort involved, so you can judge the investment before starting.

**Do the workshop six regardless.** They are the ones that pay off more than once.

**You are encouraged to email your instruction team for early feedback on your progress.**

:::note
If you or your project partner have other ideas for good activities, [ping the instructors](mailto:cs-46x-help@oregonstate.edu) with the proposed activity name and description.
:::

### The YC Startup School Curriculum

[Startup School](https://www.startupschool.org/) is a free online course on how to start a startup, by the world's top startup accelerator: Y Combinator.

It does not fit in any specific category because it touches on many different aspects of building a product and creating a company, so it is listed here.

This curriculum is useful both as a career skill-building tool and as a way to succeed with a New Product or Game project.

If you are interested in commercializing your project, consider joining a startup accelerator program. [OSU has one](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator). Other famous ones include [Station F](https://stationf.co/) in Paris (France) and [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/) in the San Francisco Bay Area.
