This section is a **practice library**: a collection of well-crafted exercises you can run with your team or on your own. None of them are graded. They exist to help you execute your project well and to prepare you for the [graded assignments](/assignments/introduction/), which are the source of truth for what is due and when.

The activities are organized by theme:

- [Career Preparation](/activities/career/): resumes, portfolios, interviews, networking.
- [Communication](/activities/communication/): presenting, writing, accessibility.
- [Conflict Resolution](/activities/conflict/): difficult behaviors, performance conversations, conflict styles.
- [Creative](/activities/creative/): ideation, brainstorming, design sprints.
- [Design](/activities/design/): architecture, prototypes, technology evaluation, security.
- [Planning](/activities/planning/): metrics, prioritization, risk, scheduling.
- [Reflective](/activities/reflective/): reading, watching, journaling, external feedback.
- [Requirements](/activities/requirements/): canvases, user stories, research questions.
- [Teamwork](/activities/teamwork/): stand-ups, team health, dysfunction assessments.
- [User](/activities/user/): finding real users, interviews, personas, UX maturity.

## How to Use This Library

**Pick what your project needs.** You are not expected to do all of these, or even most of them. A team building a game will lean on the creative and design activities; a research project will lean on requirements and user activities. Browse the themes, pick the exercises that address your current gap, and run them.

**Some activities directly prepare graded work, and they say so.** Where an activity feeds a graded assignment (for example, user-research activities feed the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) user access plan, and design activities feed your [RFC](/assignments/rfc/)), the page includes a pointer. Doing the activity first makes the graded deliverable easier and better.

**A small curated subset is offered as extra credit each term.** The instructors announce the selection in Canvas at the start of the term. The subset is deliberately small and graded pass/fail: it rewards doing the practice, not polishing a document. Each activity notes the effort involved and what a good output looks like, so you can judge the investment before you start.

**You are encouraged to email your instruction team for early feedback on your progress.**

:::note
If you or your project partner have other ideas for good activities, [ping the instructors](mailto:cs-46x-help@oregonstate.edu) with the proposed activity name and description.
:::

### The YC Startup School Curriculum

[Startup School](https://www.startupschool.org/) is a free online course on how to start a startup, by the world's top startup accelerator: Y Combinator.

It does not fit in any specific category because it touches on many different aspects of building a product and creating a company, so it is listed here.

This curriculum is useful both as a career skill-building tool and as a way to succeed with a New Product or Game project.

If you are interested in commercializing your project, consider joining a startup accelerator program. [OSU has one](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator). Other famous ones include [Station F](https://stationf.co/) in Paris (France) and [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/) in the San Francisco Bay Area.
