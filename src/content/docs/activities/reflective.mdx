The goal of reflective activities is to give you an opportunity to apply learning to your project. The habit of regular reflection also gives you concrete material for your [Term Retrospective](/assignments/term-retrospective/) and, in spring, your [Career and Individual Retrospective](/assignments/career-retrospective/).

> "The unexamined life is not worth living" -- Socrates

## Analyze a FOSS Project

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/> <Badge text="Recommended" variant="tip" class="mb-6"/>

Find a FOSS project on GitHub or another publicly available repository, ideally of some relevance to your project. Analyze what the maintainers have put in place to manage and organize the project (processes, etc.), and write a summary of what you learned that could or should be applied to your project. Include specifics. Share it with your team.

**Feeds:** the fall week 5 [repo checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/) gate, the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) target rung, and your [RFC](/assignments/rfc/) options, since upstream conventions constrain them hard.

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Watch a documentary film related to your project. Each team member writes a paragraph about a different aspect of the film that is relevant to the project.

Here are some ideas:

## External Feedback Session

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/> <Badge text="Recommended" variant="tip" class="mb-6"/>

Find someone who has relevant expertise to review your project. It can be a technical person or a knowledge domain expert. The person should have little knowledge of your project. 

Present the relevant parts of your project to them and collect their feedback. Write a summary of what you learned that could or should be applied to your project. Include specifics. Share it with your team.

**Feeds:** the [Term Retrospective](/assignments/term-retrospective/) evidence criterion, and the spring [Release and Metrics](/assignments/release/).

## Learning Journal

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/> <Badge text="Recommended" variant="tip" class="mb-6"/>

A learning journal is a personal record where you reflect on your learning experiences, capturing insights, challenges, and progress over time. It's all about metacognition (thinking about thinking).

1. Write **weekly** reflections on what you learned, challenges faced, and how you overcame them.
2. Relate new knowledge to past experiences.
3. Evaluate your skills and identify areas for improvement.

A term of journal entries is the single best source of material for your [Term Retrospective](/assignments/term-retrospective/) and the spring [Career and Individual Retrospective](/assignments/career-retrospective/): instead of reconstructing ten weeks from memory, you quote yourself.

**Feeds:** the [Term Retrospective](/assignments/term-retrospective/) individual pages, the [sprint note](/assignments/sprint-notes/) judgment layer, and the spring [Career and Individual Retrospective](/assignments/career-retrospective/).

## Keep an Experiment Log

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/> <Badge text="Recommended" variant="tip" class="mb-6"/>

Record every run you make, so that in spring you can say what you tried and what it showed instead of reconstructing six months from shell history. Ten minutes per experiment.

- **One entry per run**: date, the question it was meant to answer, the exact configuration and commit hash, the result, and what you concluded.
- **Log the failures too.** The runs that went nowhere are the ones you will otherwise repeat, and they are what a paper's limitations section is made of.
- **Note what you changed** relative to the previous run, one thing at a time wherever you can.
- **Review the log at every sprint boundary** and pull the interesting entries into your sprint note.

A good output is a running log in which any entry can be traced to a commit and re-run.

**Feeds:** the [sprint note](/assignments/sprint-notes/) what-got-done item, and the spring [Release and Metrics](/assignments/release/) evidence.
