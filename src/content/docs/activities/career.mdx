The goal of Capstone is to set you up for professional success. The Career Preparation activities encourage you to polish your career skills from different perspectives.

A note on the 2026 job market: with AI-assisted screening on the employer side and AI-assisted applications on yours, a polished resume is table stakes, not a differentiator. What stands out is **demonstrable shipped work**: a live project, a public repository with real users or real reviews, a portfolio that shows what you built and the decisions behind it. Your capstone project is exactly that kind of evidence, which is why several activities below focus on making your work visible. Interview preparation has not changed as much: most technical interviews still include live coding, so regular practice still pays off.

These activities also feed directly into the [Career and Individual Retrospective](/assignments/career-retrospective/) you write in spring: the more of them you do during the year, the more concrete material you have to reflect on.

> "Opportunities don't happen, you create them." --  Chris Grosser

## Attend a Guest Speaker Event

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Attend two guest speaker events (either from class or outside) and write a reflection on the speakers' career paths, insights shared, and how they relate to your career aspirations. List the questions you asked the speakers and the answers you received.

## Resume Building

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Create or update your resume, emphasizing relevant projects and internships. Have your resume reviewed by three (3) peers, then revise it based on their feedback.

Keep the original resume, the feedback from your three peers, and the updated version. Comparing the before and after shows you what kind of feedback moves the needle, and it makes strong retrospective material.

## Practice LeetCode

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Improve coding skills by solving at least three (3) problems on platforms like [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/). Live coding remains a standard part of technical interviews, and it does not allow AI assistance; there is no substitute for practice.

Keep a list of problems solved and a brief reflection on the concepts you learned or re-learned.

## Mock Interviews

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Participate in at least two mock interviews to build confidence and refine communication.

Write a summary of the interview experience, including strengths and areas for improvement.

## Portfolio Development

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Create or update an online portfolio showcasing your work. In an AI-assisted job market, a portfolio that shows working software and explains your decisions is worth more than a list of technologies. Have your portfolio reviewed by three (3) peers, then revise it based on their feedback.

A good output is a live portfolio link plus the peer feedback you acted on. Your capstone project belongs in it: link the repository, the running product, and (in spring) your team's landing page.

## Attend Job Fairs or Industry Networking Events

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Network with employers at least twice (2 events).

Write a summary of contacts made, companies of interest, and potential follow-ups for each event (indicate the event and date).

## LinkedIn Optimization

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Create or update and optimize your LinkedIn profile. Have your profile reviewed by three (3) peers, then revise it based on their feedback.

A good output is your updated profile link plus a short note on what you changed and why.

## Personal Branding

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Establish an online presence through blogs or social media. Create at least one blog post and one social media post to promote your blog post. A write-up about a real problem you solved on your capstone project is an excellent first post.

Write up goals for your personal branding over the short, medium, and long terms, and keep links to the blog post and social media post.

## Write a Cover Letter

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Find a job or internship of interest and write a cover letter for it. Have your letter reviewed by two (2) peers, then revise it based on their feedback. Recruiters increasingly assume cover letters are AI-generated; specificity about the company and about your own shipped work is what makes yours credible.

Keep the original job description, the peer feedback, and the final letter together; the set makes a reusable template for future applications.

## Join a Professional Association

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Join a professional association (such as ACM) and attend at least two events.

A good output is your membership confirmation and a summary of the events and the networking opportunities available.

## Complete an Online Certification Course

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Complete a course relevant to your field (e.g., AWS, Google Cloud).

Keep the certification and write a summary of new skills learned and how they apply to your career aspirations.

## Public Code Repository

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Polish and make one of your code repositories public (e.g., GitHub). Have your project reviewed by two (2) peers, then revise the repository and its README based on their feedback. A public repository with a clear README, working setup instructions, and honest documentation is one of the strongest signals you can put in front of an employer.

A good output is the repository link plus the peer feedback you acted on.

## Read Wait But Why Post on Career Picking

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Read the article [How to Pick a Career (That Actually Fits You)](https://waitbutwhy.com/2018/04/picking-career.html) to gain insights into how to approach career decisions thoughtfully. The post delves into understanding your values, exploring different career paths, and making informed, long-term choices that align with your passions and skills. This reflective activity can help you make more meaningful career decisions.

Write a reflection on how the insights influence your career planning.

## Participate in a Hackathon

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>
<Badge text="Team Activity" variant="note" class="mb-6" />

Participate in a hackathon/codefest (e.g., BeaverHacks) to develop your coding skills, teamwork, and problem-solving abilities.

Write a summary of the hackathon experience, including the project developed (with screenshots or pictures if available), challenges faced, and lessons learned.

## Job Shadowing

<Badge text="Individual Activity" variant="success" class="mb-6"/>

Spend one day observing an industry professional in your desired career path. This activity requires a little more planning to find a professional willing to host you for a day.

Write a reflection on the experience, including insights gained and how it influences your career goals. What did you learn about the day-to-day responsibilities of the role? What surprised you?
