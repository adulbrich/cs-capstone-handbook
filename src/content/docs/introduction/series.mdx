Senior Software Engineering Project I, II, and III, collectively known as **Capstone**, represent the culmination of our Computer Science students' academic journey.

Term   | ID       | Class Name | Credits
-------| ---------|------------|--------
Fall   | CS 461   | Senior Software Engineering Project I   | 3
Winter | CS 462   | Senior Software Engineering Project II  | 3
Spring | CS 463   | Senior Software Engineering Project III | 2

This three-course sequence transforms aspiring developers into industry-ready software engineers through real-world project experience and professional collaboration.

Students work in [teams](/practicalities/teams/) (generally 3-4 students) to design, develop, and deliver software solutions for real-world clients, gaining hands-on experience that bridges the gap between academic learning and professional practice.

## How We Measure Success

Each term, the grade combines four equal components: your project partner's evaluation, your teammates' peer evaluations, individual evidence that nobody can produce for you, and the team's deliverables. The [assignments overview](/assignments/introduction/#grade-architecture) has the breakdown, and the emphasis evolves across the series to mirror how a real project progresses.

## Schedule

Week by week for 2026-27, from the first Friday of fall to the last day of spring. Fall has a **week 0** because classes start on a Wednesday; winter and spring start at week 1. Dates are the Monday of each week and are refreshed every September against the registrar calendar. Sprints are two weeks long everywhere, and your cohort [check-in](/assignments/introduction/#cohort-check-ins) with your TA happens every two weeks on the schedule your TA publishes in Canvas.

The **Read** and **Do** columns run about one sprint ahead of what they prepare: read the guide and run the activity before the week the assignment is due, not during it. The assignment pages are the [source of truth](/assignments/introduction/) for what is due, when, and how it is scored; this schedule only lays them out in time. Lecture slots marked "announced in Canvas" carry guest speakers or a workshop, decided closer to the date.
