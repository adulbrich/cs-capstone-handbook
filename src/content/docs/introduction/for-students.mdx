## What Kinds of Projects Are Available?

We have four possible project categories:

- Free and Open Source Software (FOSS): contribute meaningful patches to an existing FOSS project
- Consultancy: develop a software solution for a client (company, faculty, etc.) that will be deployed to production
- New Product or Game: create a product or game and gather actual users (entrepreneurial spirit required)
- Research: work on a research hypothesis with the goal of publishing a paper

We've had some projects in the past that started as a new product then turned research, or consultancy that turned into a FOSS project. 

Sometimes, projects don't fit neatly into one category. In that case, you'll have to set custom expectations for the project outcome with your project partner and instructors.

## Are There Lectures?

Lectures are on Fridays from 10:00 AM to 11:50 AM (10:50 AM in Spring). This is an opportunity to connect with your team and instructors in person. What happens in each Friday slot, what is due that week, and what to read and do before it are on the [week-by-week schedule](/introduction/series/#schedule).

We also have guest speakers from the industry, alumni, or faculty members, and alumni who started companies are part of the mix. Our speakers will show up during lecture time, so you can ask them questions and learn from their experience. We prefer in-person speakers where possible; some sessions are online.

## What Kind of Class is This?

Compared to previous classes, the Capstone series is a medium-term team project. You will have to plan and execute work over the course of 9 months (starting in Fall term and ending in Spring term).

You *cannot* approach this class as a regular, assignment-based class. If you tackle assignments last minute, you *will not excel in this class*. Each term, your grade combines four equal components, none of which you can leave until the last week. All graded work, with the weights and the due weeks, is in the [Assignments section](/assignments/introduction/) of this handbook, which Canvas mirrors.

You haven't done much in terms of longer-term team projects, up to now. The Capstone series aims to address this shortcoming and give you the necessary skills to plan and strive under uncertainty. Depending on the project category, your success will depend solely on your capacity to adapt and deliver.

## What is IP/NDA?

IP stands for Intellectual Property. By default, you have the rights to the work you produce during Capstone. Some projects might require you to hand over the IP to your project partner or their company (and will be labeled as such). 

We encourage you to discuss IP with your project partner early in the academic year, even if the project will be open-source (see licensing below).

NDA stands for Non-Disclosure Agreement. It means that some or all of the work you'll be doing or information you will get access to cannot be shared outside of the boundaries set by the agreement. Some project partners or companies might require you to sign an NDA if you are working on proprietary or closed-source code. 

Projects have to specify if they require IP agreements or NDAs.

Instructors cannot sign an IP agreement or NDA. If your project is under NDA, we'll adjust how we assess your work: instead of reading your repository directly, the teaching staff will grade through live screen-shared walkthroughs and sanitized evidence. See the [assignments overview](/assignments/introduction/#nda-projects) for how this works.

## What About Licensing?

Licensing broadly refers to what people can do with your code or work. 

It's a good idea to discuss with your team and project partner the type of license you want for your open-source project if you are not under specific IP or NDA requirements. 

Check [choose a license](https://choosealicense.com/) for more information. You could come up with your own licensing terms. 

If you want to keep your project closed-source (because you want to commercialize it for example), discuss that as well.

## My Project Might Become a Company. Should I Keep the Idea to Myself?

No. A great idea is not worth much if you cannot execute it, and execution is what this course is for. A deployed product that strangers use is worth more to you than a secret, and it is also what the [New Product or Game](/practicalities/categories/#new-product-or-game) ladder rewards.

The path already exists in this handbook. You hold the IP by default (see above). You can keep the project closed-source if you intend to commercialize it (see licensing, above). And the [Project Handoff](/assignments/project-handoff/) allows the successor to be you. If you want help turning the project into a company, the [OSU Advantage Accelerator](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator) is the campus resource; its [Iterate workshop](/activities/requirements/#osu-advantage-accelerators-iterate-program) is in the activity library. None of that replaces a deployed URL and a dozen users you do not know.
