You are an industry professional, founder, or faculty or staff member with a project a team of 3 to 4 computer science seniors can work on for three terms (30 weeks). It costs you an hour a week per team and two evaluations each term.

## Submitting a Project

Submissions close when fall term starts, and the earlier yours arrives the more time we have to help you refine it. Students rank the approved projects in a bidding survey before the term, which is why the write-up has to sell the project.

- **Description, Problem Statement, and Objectives.** What you would hand a summer intern: a complete system or feature, off your critical path. For some of the team this is their first real project.
- **Qualifications.** Minimum and preferred, knowing that many students might not yet have taken the higher level elective classes yet.
- **IP and NDA.** Say in the proposal whether the project requires an intellectual property agreement, a non-disclosure agreement, or both. Either is fine and neither disqualifies a project; both are visible to students before they bid. [IP, NDA, and Licensing](/introduction/ip-nda-and-licensing/) has what each one changes, including why the instruction team cannot be a party to one.
- **Several projects.** Larger companies that want more than one team join [INSPiRE Industry Affiliates](https://engineering.oregonstate.edu/EECS/alumni-industry/student-engagement/inspire). Individuals are never asked for a donation.
- **No idea yet?** Talk to use or consider [mentoring](/introduction/for-mentors/) a student-proposed project. Questions about fit: [cs-46x-help@oregonstate.edu](mailto:cs-46x-help@oregonstate.edu).

## What We Expect From You

- Availability from the start of fall term to the end of spring term.
- At least one hour a week per team to meet the students and answer questions.
- Technical guidance from you or a colleague; ask us if you need a technical mentor.
- Expectations for the work, and how you will judge it, set with the students in the first sprint.
- Reviews of their work and tests of their prototypes when they ask.
- Repository read access for the instruction team by default; under an NDA the team is assessed through [live walkthroughs and sanitized evidence](/assignments/introduction/#nda-projects), same points.
- An evaluation each term through our survey, against the [project partner evaluation rubrics](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/): progress in fall and winter, outcome in spring. Where a rubric does not fit, read it against the expectations you set.

**Your evaluation is 25% of each student's grade each term**, the same weight as each of the other three components ([grade architecture](/assignments/introduction/#grade-architecture)).

## What We Do Each Term

- **Fall:** place students on your project through the bidding survey and share your contact; expect the team's first email in week 1 or early week 2, when teams are announced.
- **Every term:** send a midterm pulse survey and a final survey in the weeks the [schedule](/introduction/schedule/) shows, and run the [assignments](/assignments/introduction/) that structure the team's work.
- **Spring:** make sure you receive a proper handoff of the work that has been performed during the year.

Students owe the course about nine hours a week in fall and winter (3 credits each) and six in spring (2 credits), per student, meetings and class time included. Email us whenever a concern comes up.
