You are an industry professional, founder, or recent alumnus without a project to hand over. Many of our students propose their own projects (portfolio products, open-source contributions, startup ideas), and every one of those teams must have an approved mentor: someone who validates the direction, keeps scope from creeping, shares career judgment, and opens doors to users, collaborators, or employers.

We especially want people who shipped something recently. A mentor who launched a product three years ago is more useful to a senior than a vice president, because the students can picture being them and the advice comes from fresh decisions. Alumni who started a company or shipped a real project are wanted at any size of outcome.

## What We Expect From You

The same as from a project partner; the [full list](/introduction/for-partners/#what-we-expect-from-you) is on that page. In short:

- availability from the start of fall term to the end of spring term;
- at least one hour a week with the team;
- expectations set together with the students in the first sprint;
- an evaluation each term through the survey we send, using the [project partner evaluation rubrics](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/).

**Your evaluation is 25% of each student's grade each term**, the same weight as each of the other three components ([grade architecture](/assignments/introduction/#grade-architecture)).

Two things differ for mentors. You do not need to know every technical detail; direction, process, and professionalism are what the team lacks. And student-proposed projects rarely carry an NDA, so the teaching staff reads the repository directly and your evaluation need not verify artifacts.

## What We Do Each Term

- **Before fall term:** match you with a team on your interests and their project, and share the students' contacts and project details.
- **Every term:** send the rubrics and surveys, and stay reachable for any concern about the students or the coursework.
- **Throughout:** run the [assignments](/assignments/introduction/) that tell you what to expect from the team at each stage.
