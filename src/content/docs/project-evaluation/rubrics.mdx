Every graded assignment publishes its rubric on its own page in the [assignments section](/assignments/introduction/). This page documents the conventions those rubrics share.

## Bands

Rubrics use three bands per criterion:

- **Exceeds**: full points. The work does what the criterion asks, verifiably, and shows judgment beyond the mechanical minimum.
- **Meets**: partial points. The work substantially does what the criterion asks, with gaps that do not undermine it.
- **Does Not Meet**: low points. The work attempts the criterion but falls short in ways that matter.

**Missing is not a band.** Work that is not submitted, is inaccessible to graders (broken links, private documents without access, videos that do not play), or is off-topic scores zero on the affected criteria. Check your links from a logged-out browser before submitting.

## Evidence Over Prose

Criteria are written to be verifiable: commit hashes, CI runs, URLs, PR IDs, measured numbers. When a rubric asks for evidence, prose describing the evidence does not substitute for it. NDA (Track B) teams follow the sanitized-evidence conventions on the [assignments overview](/assignments/introduction/#nda-projects-track-a-and-track-b).

## Sprint Notes Are Pass/Fail

[Sprint notes](/assignments/sprint-notes/) are deliberately not banded: each checklist item is present and real, or it is not. Rubric energy is reserved for the term deliverables and individual evidence.

## Learning Outcome Tags

Each rubric criterion carries a tag (`SO1` through `SO6`, `L07` through `L10`) naming the learning outcome it evidences. The tags exist for accreditation traceability, are verified in CI against each page's rubric table and the coverage minimums (see `scripts/validate-outcomes.mjs`), and change nothing about how students complete the work. The [outcome mapping](/learning-objectives/mapping/) summarizes them.

## Canvas

Rubrics are authored here and imported into Canvas (the TSV sources live in the course repository). If Canvas and this handbook ever disagree, the handbook wins; report the discrepancy.
