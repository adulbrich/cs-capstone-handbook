CS 462 is the Writing Intensive Course (WIC) of the capstone series. We don't want to make you write for the sake of writing, so the WIC learning outcomes are evidenced by the same work that evidences the ABET ones. All the writing happens through ordinary software engineering activities: design decisions, sprint evidence, reviews, and a postmortem.

## Learning Outcomes

Students in WIC courses will [[1]](#wic-ref):

1. Analyze disciplinary concepts through writing.
2. Create at least one original, extensive, and meaningful written work that adheres to disciplinary norms including specified audience, purpose, and genre.
3. Integrate feedback after critical evaluation during the revision process of written work.

## How CS 462 Does the Writing

The extensive written work is the winter [RFC](/assignments/rfc/): an individual design document of at least 2,000 words in its revised form, argued from cited sources, written as a draft, reviewed in writing by two students from other teams, and revised with a response-to-feedback note; staff grade the final against the rubric. Around it sits the informal writing: your contribution line and the team's judgment layer in every [sprint note](/assignments/sprint-notes/), your two written peer reviews, and the [incident postmortem](/assignments/incident-postmortem/). The audiences differ on purpose: the RFC is written for technical peers and staff, the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) and the postmortem for your project partner. Every assignment states what AI may do on it and what would fail it, which is the disciplinary norm the [AI policy](/assignments/introduction/#ai-policy-applies-to-every-assignment) sets. Where each outcome is evidenced, per student, is on the [outcome mapping page](/learning-objectives/mapping/).
