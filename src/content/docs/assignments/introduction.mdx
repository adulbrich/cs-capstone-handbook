## Submitting Your Work

Every document you submit is one PDF. Its first lines give the document title, your project ID and name, and each author's name and email: every team member on team work, only you on individual work. Type them at the top of your Markdown before you convert it, for example:

```markdown
# Team Charter

Project 42: Beaver Habitat Tracker

Alex Ulbrich <ulbrical@oregonstate.edu>, Jane Doe <doej@oregonstate.edu>
```

When one submission combines several files, such as the three of the [Team Charter](/assignments/team-charter/), the cover lines appear once, at the top of the first. Any tool that turns Markdown into a PDF works, such as the VS Code "Markdown PDF" extension or printing the rendered file from a browser. The PDF shows the rendered document, not raw Markdown: organized under real headings, with real tables, diagrams as images rather than their source, and alt text on every image.

A repository link does not replace the PDF, even when the document also lives in your repository, because the PDF is the version graded and kept. Three submissions are exceptions and say so on their pages: the [Introduction Email](/assignments/term-startup/#fall-week-1-introduction-email) is a screenshot of the sent message, the [Landing Page](/assignments/landing-page/) is a URL and a screenshot, and the [Release and Metrics](/assignments/release/) trailer is a video beside its PDF report.

If your project is under an NDA, [IP, NDA, and Licensing](/introduction/ip-nda-and-licensing/#teams-under-an-nda) has the one rule for what an NDA team submits, and each affected page adds a short note.

## Rubric Conventions

Most rubrics use three bands per criterion:

| Band | Points | The work |
|---|---|---|
| Exceeds | Full | Does what the criterion asks, verifiably, and shows judgment beyond the mechanical minimum. |
| Meets | Partial | Substantially does what the criterion asks, with gaps that do not undermine it. |
| Does Not Meet | Low | Attempts the criterion but falls short in ways that matter. |

Work that is not submitted, is off-topic, or cannot be opened by a grader (broken links, private documents, videos that do not play) scores zero on the affected criteria. "Does Not Meet" is only for work you submitted that falls short. The [Defense](/assignments/defense/) is the one rubric that carries an explicit **Missing** rating at zero, for an unexcused no-show. Check every link from a logged-out browser before you submit.

Criteria are written to be verifiable: commit hashes, CI runs, URLs, PR IDs, measured numbers. Describing the evidence does not substitute for linking it.

The rubrics that use other bands:

- Each [sprint note](/assignments/sprint-notes/) item is present and real (Pass, full points) or it is not (Fail, zero).
- [Workshop Activities](/assignments/workshop-activities/) and [Term Startup](/assignments/term-startup/) are Complete or Incomplete.
- Each sprint's [individual contribution](/assignments/sprint-notes/#sprint-notes-n-individual-contribution) is Full, Partial, or Zero.
- The peer and partner surveys score on the scales their rubrics give.

Rubric criteria also carry learning outcome tags, `SO1` through `SO6` and `L07` through `L10`. They record where each outcome is evidenced and change nothing about how you complete the work; [grading](/learning-objectives/grading/#learning-outcome-tags) and the [outcome mapping](/learning-objectives/mapping/) explain them. How points become a letter grade is on the same [grading page](/learning-objectives/grading/).

## Late Work, Absence, and Makeup

Submissions are due at the posted time. Planned conflicts (interviews, travel) need at least 48 hours' notice.

| Work | If it's late or missed |
|---|---|
| Most submissions | Up to 48 hours late loses one rubric band on each criterion (Exceeds grades as Meets, and so on). Beyond 48 hours it scores zero, unless you contacted the instructors before the deadline with a documented reason. |
| Sprint notes | Not accepted late: a note is pass/fail evidence of the sprint that just happened, so a missed note scores zero. One missed note with a documented reason per term is excused on request. |
| Workshops | Credit only when done in class or by the end of the week the class happened. A [workshop](/assignments/workshop-activities/) submitted after that week scores zero. |
| Defense | If you're sick or absent, tell your TA before the session and you'll be rescheduled that term, with no penalty. An unexcused no-show scores zero on your individual defense. |
| Demos | Send your TA a video of your segment within 48 hours, recorded as the [demo cadence](/assignments/sprint-notes/#demo-cadence) describes; it costs you nothing. Every check-in carries its own demo, so there is no spare slot to reschedule into. |
| Peer and partner surveys | The completion penalties are on their pages. A survey can't be made up after it closes, because the results feed grade computation. |

For illness and emergencies, contact the instructors as soon as you reasonably can. The instructors will always find a path for a documented emergency; the one case they cannot recover is one they were never told about.

## AI Policy (applies to every assignment)

Generative AI tools may be used on any assignment unless a page says otherwise. Three standing rules:

1. **You own the result.** Correctness, security, licensing, and professionalism of anything you submit are your responsibility, whatever produced it. [choosealicense.com](https://choosealicense.com/) summarizes what the common licenses permit and require, which is where to start when a dependency carries one you don't recognize.
2. **Delegate deliberately, and build the checks that make it reasonable.** Major deliverables ask what you handed to AI tools, what checks made that reasonable (tests, CI, review gates, staging, a rollback path), and where you stopped to validate the result yourself. Validate carefully where a decision is hard to reverse: architecture, database schema, authentication, deployment, data migrations, licensing. Delegate freely where your checks catch mistakes cheaply. What is graded is the fit between how far you delegated and how strong your checks are, never how sophisticated your tooling is, so a weaker model or a tighter budget is not a disadvantage: it simply means tighter gates and more validation of your own.
3. **Respect the confidentiality boundary.** What may be pasted into which tools is defined in your Team Charter's AI and confidentiality section, per your partner agreement. See the [Generative AI guide](/guides/generative-ai/).

The [AI practice activities](/activities/ai/) teach all three of these, including how to tell a hard-to-reverse decision from an easy-to-reverse one and how to build checks you can actually trust.

## TA Check-ins

Every team has a Teaching Assistant (TA), with a standing 25-to-30-minute check-in in each week the [Schedule](/introduction/schedule/) marks. The fall week 2 check-in goes through your [Term Startup](/assignments/term-startup/) list while it is still in progress. Check announcements in week 1 for your TA; your team and your TA agree the check-in time. The check-in is where your [sprint demo](/assignments/sprint-notes/#demo-cadence) happens, live, with every student showing a piece of their own work from the sprint. The [Defense](/assignments/defense/), once per term, is a separate session in week 7 or 9; check announcements for the schedule. Between check-ins, your TA is reachable async.
