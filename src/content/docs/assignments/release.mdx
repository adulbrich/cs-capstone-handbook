**Team submission. Spring, week 8. 8% of the spring grade.**

This is the permanent, shareable record of what your team built: an 8-to-12-minute video presenting the project to a general audience, plus one page of release notes. It is a portfolio artifact you will reference in interviews, and it is where you show, with real numbers, whether the project did what your [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) said it would. You have demoed live at least a dozen times by now; this is the polished cut.

## The Video (8 to 12 minutes)

Upload to media.oregonstate.edu as unlisted (public platforms in addition are welcome). Closed captions or a transcript required. The [Presentations guide](/guides/presentations/) covers structure and delivery.

**Problem statement (1 to 2 minutes).** What problem, stated concretely; who experiences it and what it costs them; why it was not already solved; what your team's specific scope was and what you deliberately left out. Support with visuals, not a talking head.

**Showcase (5 to 7 minutes).** The project in action. Product and tool teams: start from a realistic user entry point and walk one or two complete use cases end to end. Research and pipeline teams: run the pipeline or analysis end to end and present findings a non-specialist can follow. Narrate clearly, highlight the technically interesting parts, and say plainly which features are partial. No silence longer than five seconds.

**Metrics and outcomes (2 to 3 minutes).** The segment that separates a release from a demo:

- Restate the **2 or 3 success metrics** from `docs/shipped.md`.
- Report a **real measured value for at least one**, with instrumentation evidence on screen: an analytics screenshot, benchmark output, download or install counts, user task results. Winter's instrumentation commitment comes due here.
- Give an **honest outcomes assessment** with concrete numbers against your category's bar: delivered versus the Definition of Shipped, what is fully working, partial, or descoped, patches accepted, users reached, deployment status, or findings produced.
- Close with the one or two most important lessons and the highest-priority next step.

## Release Notes (one page, PDF)

Project name, team, and date; the video link with **timestamps for each segment**; a one-paragraph summary; features or findings delivered; one line of measured outcome ("42 installs, 8 of 10 study participants completed the core task"); known limitations; and the repository or archive link. Test every link in a private browser window before submitting; a link graders cannot open is a zero on the affected criteria.

## Rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Problem statement: concrete, audience-appropriate, with cost to the user and explicit scope | 15 | SO3 |
| Showcase: complete use case or pipeline end to end, realistic entry point, honest about partial features | 25 | SO2 |
| Metrics evidence: metrics match `docs/shipped.md`, at least one reported with a real measured value and visible instrumentation evidence | 30 | SO2 |
| Honest outcomes: delivered versus the Definition of Shipped with concrete numbers, lessons, and a prioritized next step | 20 | SO6 |
| Release notes and production quality: complete one-pager with timestamps and working links; clear narration, captions or transcript, within 8 to 12 minutes | 10 | L08 |

**AI use:** AI may help with scripting, slides, and editing. Fabricating demo footage, metrics, findings, or user feedback fails the assignment; a smaller true number always beats a bigger invented one. The outcomes segment is graded on honesty, and "we did not reach the bar, here is exactly how far we got and why" scores well.

:::note[NDA teams]
Agree with your partner in advance on what may appear on screen. A redacted or sanitized showcase is fine; most partners approve a version once they see the plan. If nothing public is possible, your instructor arranges a private review of the same material against the same rubric.
:::
