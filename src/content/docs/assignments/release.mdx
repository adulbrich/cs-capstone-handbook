Two artifacts, each doing what its medium is good at: a **3-minute trailer** that proves the software runs and that someone might actually watch, and a **2-page report** carrying the numbers. Three minutes is short enough that a recruiter, a future teammate, or your partner's boss watches to the end, and measured results belong in text, where they can be checked, quoted in a resume, and read again next year.

You have demoed every sprint for three terms. This is the polished cut of the one flow that matters.

## The Trailer (3 minutes, hard cap)

Upload to [media.oregonstate.edu](https://media.oregonstate.edu/) as unlisted; public platforms in addition are welcome. [Closed captions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/) are required. WCAG 2.2 asks for them on prerecorded video at Level A and a transcript alone does not satisfy that, which is why the rubric grades captions and not a transcript. They also make the video work with the sound off. The [Presentations guide](/guides/presentations/) covers structure and delivery.

Three minutes forces one decision: which single thing do you show? Answer it before you record.

- **The problem, in one sentence** (about 20 seconds). Who has it and what it costs them. No preamble, no team introductions, no agenda slide.
- **The software running** (about 2 minutes 10). One complete flow end to end from a realistic entry point. Product and tool teams: a real user task. Research and pipeline teams: the pipeline running on real data and producing the result. Narrate what is happening. No silence longer than five seconds, and no slides where you could show the thing itself.
- **What it achieved, in one line** (about 20 seconds). One real number, and where the report is.

Say plainly if a feature shown is partial. Fabricated footage fails the assignment.

## The Report (2 pages)

This is the graded evidence and the durable artifact.

1. **Problem and scope**: the problem concretely, who experiences it, and what your team deliberately left out.
2. **Delivered**: the features, findings, or merged contributions that exist, with what is fully working, partial, or descoped stated as such.
3. **Metrics**: restate the 2 or 3 success metrics from your [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) (the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) covers the rung those metrics were chosen against). Report a **real measured value for at least one**, with instrumentation evidence embedded: an analytics screenshot, benchmark output, install counts, user task results. Winter's instrumentation commitment comes due here.
4. **Delivered versus the Definition of Shipped**: your target rung, where you actually landed, and the gap with concrete numbers. Patches accepted, users reached, deployment status, findings produced.
5. **Limitations and next step**: the one or two most important lessons and the highest-priority thing a successor should do.
6. **Links**: the trailer, the repository or archive, the [Landing Page](/assignments/landing-page/), and the release tag. Test every link in a private browser window before submitting; a link graders cannot open scores zero on the affected criteria.

## What You Submit

The report as one PDF, linking the trailer uploaded as [The Trailer](#the-trailer-3-minutes-hard-cap) describes; its first lines carry the cover [Submitting Your Work](/assignments/introduction/#submitting-your-work) describes.

Fabricating demo footage, metrics, findings, or user feedback fails the assignment; a smaller true number always beats a bigger invented one. The outcomes section is graded under "Accurate outcomes", and "we did not reach the bar, here is exactly how far we got and why" scores well.
