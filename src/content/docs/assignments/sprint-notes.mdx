**Team submission with the individual contribution modifier. Every term: 4 notes in fall and winter (2% each), 2 in spring. Pass/fail per checklist item.**

At the end of every two-week sprint, the team submits a **one-page** sprint note and, on the demo cadence below, shows working software live. There is no long-form progress report in this course. The note is evidence plus judgment, not prose: if your repository and board are in order, producing it takes 15 minutes.

You may generate the evidence sections with AI from your own repo history and board. The judgment layer must be written by humans; it is the part we actually read. Start from the [sprint note template](/sprint-note-template.md).

## The Sprint Note (one page, five items)

1. **Working software evidence**: a URL, tagged release, or 3-minute demo video, plus the CI run. If nothing works yet, say so plainly and say why.
2. **Planned versus done**: link to the board; carried-over items named, not hidden.
3. **Top risk and one decision needed**: the single biggest threat to shipping (in fall, before your [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) exists, the biggest threat to the term gate), and one concrete decision the team needs from the partner, the staff, or itself.
4. **Contribution lines**: one line per student listing the reviewed PR(s) they owned this sprint (titles or IDs; Track B teams list IDs only), and, for whoever chaired a decision this sprint, which decision they drove to a conclusion. Across a term every student should appear at least once as the decision chair; this is the record your [defense](/assignments/defense/) role criterion is checked against, so it saves you from having to reconstruct your own leadership from memory in week 8.
5. **Judgment layer** (about five lines, human-written): what the team learned this sprint and what it changes about the plan.

## Demo Cadence

- Every team demos **live at least twice per term** in its cohort check-in (5 minutes: working software, or an honest account of why not).
- Sprints without a live slot attach a **3-minute video demo** to the sprint note. Partners are welcome to both.
- Two **all-hands demo days** per term (around weeks 5 and 10) feature a sampled lineup in lecture, with partners invited.

## Grading

Each note is checked pass/fail per item (20 points each, 100 total). A missing item scores zero; there are no bands. This is deliberate: the note is a professional habit, not an essay.

**Individual contribution modifier:** half of each sprint note's points are individual and default to full credit. A student's individual half is reduced when the sprint's evidence (contribution lines, repo record, review activity) shows no meaningful contribution. Deductions are flagged by the cohort TA with the evidence noted, and confirmed by an instructor following the corroboration process on the [partner evaluation page](/project-evaluation/project-partner-evaluation/); no deduction lands without a written evidence note you can see and respond to. You have five business days from notification to respond with evidence; an instructor makes the final call. Missing your PR-per-sprint norm once with an explanation in the note is normal life; a pattern with no explanation is what this modifier exists for.

:::tip[Why demos]
A report can be embellished; a demo cannot. Twelve reps of demoing before the Expo is also the only way the Expo presentation stops being scary. Partners consistently say the teams that demo often are the teams they trust.
:::

## Activities That Prepare This

Ten sprint notes a year means per-note recommendations would be silly. Three activities are worth doing once, in fall, and then never again: [Kanban Board Setup](/activities/planning/#kanban-board-setup), [Definition of Done](/activities/planning/#definition-of-done), and [Regular Stand-Up Meetings](/activities/teamwork/#regular-stand-up-meetings). Get those right and the note takes fifteen minutes every time.

Two are worth repeating: [Prioritization](/activities/planning/#prioritization) for the top-risk-and-decision item, and [Learning Journal](/activities/reflective/#learning-journal) for the judgment layer, which is the part staff actually read.
