Every two-week [sprint](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) ends with a one-page sprint note, due by Sunday of the sprint's second week, and a TA check-in that week where each student shows their own work. The note is evidence plus judgment, not prose: if your repository and tracker are in order, producing it takes about 15 minutes. The [sprint guide](/guides/sprint/) covers how to plan, run, and review a sprint; the windows below are when this course's sprints fall.

| Term | Sprints | Sprint windows |
|---|---|---|
| Fall | 1 to 4 | Weeks 3 to 4, 5 to 6, 7 to 8, 9 to 10 |
| Winter | 5 to 9 | Weeks 1 to 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 6, 7 to 8, 9 to 10 |
| Spring | 10 to 12 | Weeks 1 to 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 6 |

## What You Produce

The sprint note is one page with four items. Start from the [sprint note template](/sprint-note-template.md), which has every field.

1. [Working software evidence](#working-software-evidence)
2. [What got done, and who](#what-got-done-and-who)
3. [Top risk and decisions](#top-risk-and-decisions)
4. [Judgment layer](#judgment-layer)

Each sprint, also update the [living docs](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/#what-you-produce) for what the sprint changed, starting with `docs/requirements.md`, `docs/design.md`, and `docs/risks.md`. In fall and winter the next repo checkpoint checks them; in spring they go into the [Project Handoff](/assignments/project-handoff/#4-the-living-docs).

**AI use:** you may generate the note with AI from your repo history and board. Two parts must be human-written: the judgment layer, and each student's How we checked it sentence.

### Working software evidence

A URL, tagged release, or video of the software running, plus the CI run when one exists. If nothing works yet, say so and why. For example:

- No code yet, because the partner wants research or design settled first; the note links the requirements draft or architecture the partner approved, [ADRs](/guides/adr/), and notes from interviews with their staff.
- No working software yet: the open-source project you contribute to builds and passes its tests on every member's machine, and the first PR, a documentation fix, is in review.
- A clickable prototype only, because access to the partner's system hasn't been granted; the request is linked.

### What got done, and who

Every student owns at least one Done entry each sprint. Write two lists, as table rows or subheadings; one student can own several entries.

**Done** is work completed this sprint, code or not, such as a merged feature, a partner agreement reached, a dataset cleared for use, or a user session run and written up. Each entry has:

- **What now exists**, linked to its proof of completion: the PR approved and merged, the issue or ticket closed with its outcome, or the document reviewed and approved; project partner approvals count. Code also meets your team's [Definition of Done](/assignments/team-charter/#definition-of-done).
- **Its owner**, the student who authored or drove it, and **who reviewed and accepted it**.
- **How we checked it**, on the Done entry each student marks as their most consequential: one sentence the owner writes, naming the test or run, or for other work what it was checked against and who confirmed it.

**In progress**, optional, is anything else the team has taken far along: what is being built, linked, its owner, and its status ("in review", "about 80% done").

Leave off both lists:

- activity with no result, such as "worked on the API" or "researched options";
- work finished in an earlier sprint;
- routine upkeep, such as a formatter run, a lockfile bump, a rename, or moving a ticket on the board.

Link the PR, issue, ticket, or document itself, not a board card or a commit list. End with one line naming any student who owns nothing on either list.

For a routine approval, such as a plain "LGTM", name the reviewer and nothing more. When the review of a Done entry took judgment, the reviewer adds a note: the change they asked for or what they checked. If an AI reviewer also ran, the note says how it was prompted and what was left for human review. [Defense](/assignments/defense/#rubric) assessors read these notes.

### Top risk and decisions

- **Top risk**: the risk ranked first in your `docs/risks.md`, however small, linked at the sprint's last commit.
- **Decision needed**: one decision, who makes it (the partner, the instruction team, or the team), and a link to where it is being made; or "no decision is needed this sprint". Don't invent one.
- **Previous decision**: what the last note's decision became and who chaired it, meaning who drove it to a conclusion, or why it is still open. You may add other decisions concluded this sprint and who chaired them. Over a term every student chairs at least one, as your [charter](/assignments/team-charter/#1-roles) commits to, and your [defense](/assignments/defense/) role criterion checks this record.

### Judgment layer

About five human-written lines on what the team learned this sprint and what it changes about the plan, or why the plan holds.

## The Sprint Check-In

In the week each sprint note is due, your team meets your TA for its [check-in](/assignments/introduction/#ta-check-ins). Each student takes two to three minutes to open one entry they own, on either list, show the change, and run it. It can't be what you showed last time: nothing new to show means nothing was done that sprint. Your TA asks one question about it, such as which lines are yours or what your reviewer asked you to change. The rest of the check-in goes to your top risk and the decision you need.

The check-in before each [repo checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/) also goes over what that checkpoint expects.

If you miss the check-in, or your TA can't meet that week, record your part within 48 hours of the scheduled time on [media.oregonstate.edu](https://media.oregonstate.edu/): captions on, shared unlisted, one timestamp per student. Link it in the sprint note, or in a comment on your submission if the note is already in.

## What You Submit

:::note[If your project is under NDA]
Where a grader can't open the work, describe each entry in place of its link, with partner specifics replaced by neutral placeholders, as the [sanitized-version rule](/introduction/ip-nda-and-licensing/#teams-under-an-nda) says. At the check-in, show your TA the work itself on a shared screen; a described entry you didn't show there doesn't check out.
:::

### Sprint Notes N

The sprint note as one PDF, with the cover [Submitting Your Work](/assignments/introduction/#submitting-your-work) describes, and each student's part of [the sprint check-in](#the-sprint-check-in).

### Sprint Notes N: Individual Contribution

Nothing to hand in: your entries, reviewer notes, and How we checked it sentence in the sprint note are the evidence.

Your individual contribution defaults to Full, and one Done entry scores Full whatever else of yours is still in progress; a claim that doesn't check out can still score Zero. Being blocked doesn't excuse a sprint with nothing, unless the whole team is blocked.

## Rubric

### Sprint Notes N

Each note is checked pass/fail per criterion, 20 points each, and two criteria, What got done and Ownership and review, grade the same two lists; the [rubric conventions](/assignments/introduction/#rubric-conventions) list it among the rubrics that use other bands.

## Activities That Prepare This

Sprint notes repeat twelve times a year, so these recommendations aren't per note. Three activities are worth doing once, in fall: [Kanban Board Setup](/activities/team-and-workflow/#kanban-board-setup), [Definition of Done](/activities/team-and-workflow/#definition-of-done), and [Regular Stand-Up Meetings](/activities/team-and-workflow/#regular-stand-up-meetings).

Two are worth repeating: [Prioritization](/activities/requirements/#prioritization) for the top risk and decisions item, and [Learning Journal](/activities/learning-and-reflection/#learning-journal) for the judgment layer, which is the part the instruction team reads.

Browse [Team and Workflow](/activities/team-and-workflow/) for anything touching the board or estimates, [Requirements](/activities/requirements/) for prioritizing, and [Planning and Risk](/activities/planning-and-risk/) for risk.
