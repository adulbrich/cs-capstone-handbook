In this assignment you will make contact with your project partner or mentor, hold or schedule your first meeting of the term, and confirm in Canvas that the checklist below is done. It carries no points. Your interactions with your project partner are [25% of your grade every term](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/), and where your team has no partner a mentor, an instructor, or your TA stands in. Every item below is something a later graded deliverable assumes you settled in week 1.

Teams are announced in week 1 and your first meeting may land in week 2. Send the email in week 1 anyway: the meeting you are asking for has to be scheduled around somebody else's calendar, and the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/), the [week 5 checkpoint gate](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/#term-gates), and the approvals with lead times measured in weeks all start from this conversation.

## The Checklist

Every term:

1. **Kickoff email sent.** In fall you are introducing yourselves to people you have not met: start from the [introduction email template](/email-template.txt), which already carries the questions worth asking before you meet, and upload a screenshot of the sent email with your Canvas confirmation. In winter and spring nobody has changed, so it is one short paragraph confirming that the meeting cadence still holds and naming what this term is for. No screenshot after fall.
2. **First meeting held or scheduled**, with a meeting cadence and a communication channel you both agreed. Your partner committed to about an hour a week, so agree what that hour is for.
3. **Evaluations confirmed.** Confirm with them that they are the person who completes your [partner evaluations](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/) this term, and walk them through the facet lists on that page. They score you against those lists in week 6 and week 10, so a partner who has read them in week 1 is scoring you against something you both saw.
4. **Outcome expectations discussed**: what this project is for, what counts as delivered, and how ambitious the target is. You do not have to settle it in week 1. You do have to have started it, because you and your partner [agree the outcome type and the target rung](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) and record them in your Definition of Shipped.

Fall only:

5. **Repository access settled.** The default is that teaching staff have read access to your repository. Say so in this conversation. If your partner cannot allow it, tell your Teaching Assistant (TA) in week 1 and your checkpoints run as [walkthroughs instead](/assignments/introduction/#nda-projects), for the same points.
6. **If other teams are on this project**, what your team owns: which interface, repository, or milestone, and how the teams talk to each other. Agree it with the partner rather than assuming it, and write it into your Definition of Shipped.

## What You Submit

One short confirmation in Canvas: the date you emailed, the date of the first meeting or the date it is scheduled for, who attended, and your agreed cadence and channel. In fall, attach the screenshot of the sent email. A few lines. Nobody grades the writing and there is no rubric.

Your TA check-in in fall week 2 uses this checklist as its agenda, so bring what you learned rather than a document about it.

**Partner has not replied?** Two documented attempts (email plus one other channel) and then tell your TA, which is the same rule the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) uses. A partner who goes quiet is a problem staff can solve in week 2 and cannot solve in week 9.

:::note[If your project is under NDA]
Nothing in this checklist needs partner-confidential material. The Canvas confirmation states dates, attendance, and your cadence, which are not covered by an NDA. Item 5 is the conversation where you find out an NDA applies at all.
:::
