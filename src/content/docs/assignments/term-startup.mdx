In this assignment your team starts the term with your project partner or mentor: you work through this term's list below, then submit a short report of where each item stands. In fall the introduction email comes first, as a submission of its own. Your interactions with your project partner are [25% of your grade every term](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/), and where your team has no partner, your mentor (sometimes an instructor) stands in. Every item on a list is something a later graded deliverable assumes you have started.

## What to Do

Do everything on your term's list by the deadline. When an item waits on an answer from your partner, asking is your part: report it as not done yet, with the date you asked.

### Fall Week 1: Introduction Email

Teams are announced in the week 1 class session. By the end of week 1, send the introduction email to your project partner, or to your mentor where your team has no partner, with every teammate on Cc. Start from the [introduction email template](/email-template.txt), which already carries the questions worth asking before you meet. Send it as soon as you know your team: the meeting you're asking for has to fit somebody else's calendar, and the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/), the [week 5 checkpoint gate](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/#repo-checkpoint-1), and any approval with a lead time measured in weeks all start from this conversation.

If every teammate already meets with the person this email goes to, as when your team recruited the mentor for its own proposal or already works with the partner, don't send the email: message your instructors at [cs-46x-help@oregonstate.edu](mailto:cs-46x-help@oregonstate.edu) by the end of week 1, and the Introduction Email is excused. Fall item 1 of the status report is then done, with the date you last met. If even one teammate hasn't met them yet, send the email, because it is that teammate's introduction.

### Fall (by the end of week 2)

1. **Partner replied**, or, if not, your Teaching Assistant (TA) told after the two documented attempts the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/#what-you-produce) asks for. A partner who goes quiet is a problem the instruction team can solve in week 2 and can't solve in week 9.
2. **First meeting held or scheduled.**
3. **Meeting cadence and channel agreed.** Your partner committed to about an hour a week, so agree what that hour is for.
4. **Evaluator confirmed.** Confirm with your partner that they are the person who completes your [partner evaluations](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/) this term, and walk them through the facet lists on that page. They score you against those lists in week 6 and week 10.
5. **Outcome conversation started**: what this project is for, what counts as delivered, and how ambitious the target is. You don't have to settle it now, because you and your partner [agree what shipped means](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) and record it in your Definition of Shipped later in the year.
6. **Repository access settled.** The default is that the instruction team has read access to your repository. If your partner cannot allow it, tell your TA, and your checkpoints run as [walkthroughs instead](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/), for the same points.
7. **If other teams share your project**, what your team owns: which interface, repository, or milestone, and how the teams talk to each other. Agree it with your partner rather than assuming it, and write it into your Definition of Shipped.
8. **Production target raised**: ask your partner what "deployed" will mean at the fall week 10 checkpoint: where your system will run, who owns the account or infrastructure, and what approval that needs. On a free and open-source (FOSS) project it means your first pull request upstream, so ask who reviews it; on a research project, a run on sample data, so ask which data you may use. "There will be no deployment" is a fine answer. You don't have to have it agreed now: the answer you settle on is recorded for [Repo Checkpoint 1](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/#repo-checkpoint-1) in week 5.

Your TA check-in in fall week 2 falls before this deadline. Bring the list as it stands: who has replied, what is scheduled, and what is stuck.

### Winter (by the end of week 1)

1. **First meeting of the term held or scheduled.**
2. **Cadence and channel**: confirmed as they were, or the new ones.
3. **Evaluator confirmed** for this term. If the person changed, walk the new evaluator through the facet lists.
4. **Definition of Shipped reviewed**: your partner has seen your draft, or you have a date to review it together before it is due in week 3.

### Spring (by the end of week 1)

1. **First meeting of the term held or scheduled.**
2. **Cadence and channel**: confirmed as they were, or the new ones.
3. **Evaluator confirmed** for this term. If the person changed, walk the new evaluator through the facet lists.
4. **Release target and handoff recipient confirmed**: what ships by the [Release and Metrics](/assignments/release/) deadline, and who receives the project at the [Project Handoff](/assignments/project-handoff/), both in week 8.
5. **Partner invited to the [Engineering Expo](/assignments/expo/)** in week 10.

## What You Submit

### Introduction Email

One PDF per team, printed from the sent message in your mail app. It shows the sent timestamp, From, To, the Cc line with every teammate, the subject, and the whole body. It needs no cover lines, since the headers carry the names. A reply is not required; fall item 1 of the status report tracks it. A team excused under [Fall Week 1](#fall-week-1-introduction-email) submits nothing here.

### Term Startup

A short text entry, one line per item on your term's list, numbered as the list is: done, with the date; not done yet, with what happens next and by when; or, for fall item 7, does not apply. For example, in winter:

```text
1. Done: met Tuesday of week 1.
2. Done: same cadence, Tuesdays at 3, same Slack channel.
3. Not done yet: asked Wednesday of week 1 who evaluates this term; following up at Tuesday's meeting.
4. Done: draft review set with the partner for Monday of week 2.
```

It is graded complete or incomplete, never on the writing. In winter and spring, your week 2 TA check-in picks up any item still not done.

:::note[If your project is under NDA]
Nothing on these lists needs partner-confidential material. The report states dates, names, and your cadence, which an NDA doesn't cover. Fall item 6 is the conversation where you find out an NDA applies at all.
:::
