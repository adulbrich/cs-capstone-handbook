In this assignment you will write your team's operating agreement: who owns what, how decisions get made, what has to be true before code merges, and what happens when the team isn't working. It is due in week 2, before you have had the arguments it settles. The [Working Agreement guide](/guides/working-agreement/) explains why teams write one and has patterns you can adopt; this page says what you submit.

The charter lives in your repository as `docs/charter.md`. Git history is the change log; there's no separate revision table to maintain. When the charter changes (and it should, as your retrospectives teach you things), change the file and commit.

## What You Must Produce

Three artifacts, all in the repository:

1. **`docs/charter.md`**: the charter itself, 2 to 4 pages of focused, scannable policy (sections below).
2. **[`CONTRIBUTING.md`](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/setting-up-your-project-for-healthy-contributions/setting-guidelines-for-repository-contributors)**: setup steps a new contributor can follow, local commands for tests and linting, the branch and PR workflow, code review expectations, and where to ask for help. Start from the [CONTRIBUTING template](/CONTRIBUTING-template.md).
3. **The AI context file** (`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, or your tooling's equivalent): project context, conventions, and constraints your AI tools should know. If your docs are good, this file mostly points at them. [`AGENTS.md`](https://agents.md/) is the cross-tool convention and the safest default on a team where people use different tools; per-tool files should point at it rather than duplicate it.

Submit one PDF containing all three files, built as [Submitting Your Work](/assignments/introduction/#submitting-your-work) describes, with your team name and every student's name and email on the cover. A repository link does not replace it.

## Required Charter Sections

### 1. Roles

Three concerns must each have exactly one named owner. The charter names the owner of each, and says when rotation happens (at minimum, once per term). These three names are the ones the [defense](/assignments/defense/) asks about; do not rename them in your charter, whatever the [working agreement guide](/guides/working-agreement/#roles-and-ownership) says about labels being optional. The guide is worth reading for what each of the three concerns covers:

- **Project Manager (PM)**: owns the board and the partner communication cadence; owes the team an accurate view of where the sprint actually stands.
- **AI Coordinator**: owns the AI context file and the confidentiality boundary; owes the team current tooling guidance and a working answer to "can I paste this into that?"
- **Quality Owner**: owns CI health, the Definition of Done, and releases; owes the team a repository that's always one step from a demo.

State what each role owes the team in your own words. A new member should be able to read this section and know who to ask about what.

Owners are counted per concern, not per person, so team size does not change the requirement. On a team of two, one person owns two of the three concerns and the charter names which. On a team of five, the students who hold none of the three name an ownership area instead: a subsystem, the test suite, the deployment, documentation currency, or facilitation. Every student owns something nameable.

Commit to a rotation such that **every student chairs at least one decision per term** and holds a named role or comparable ownership area at least once across the year. Chairing means driving a decision to a conclusion, not merely having an opinion in the thread. Record who chaired what in each [sprint note](/assignments/sprint-notes/); that record is what the [defense](/assignments/defense/) role criterion is checked against.

### 2. Decision Process

How choices are proposed, decided, and escalated: your decision rule (consent, majority, PM tie-break), where decisions get recorded (see the [ADR guide](/guides/adr/), and [adr.github.io](https://adr.github.io/) for templates), and at least one explicit escalation or override rule for when you are stuck.

### 3. Cadence and Tools

Meeting times, communication channels, and core tools, including where decisions and notes live so an absent teammate can catch up without asking.

### 4. Conflict and Accountability

A restorative, stepwise conflict path: name it early, talk directly, involve the team, escalate to the instruction team, with time bounds and measurable triggers (missed meetings, unreviewed PR backlog). Accountability ties to observable evidence (PRs, reviews, attendance), not testimony. The [Working Agreement guide](/guides/working-agreement/) has conflict-path patterns you can adopt.

### 5. Definition of Done

The checks a change must pass before it merges: tests, review, docs updated, lint and security checks, and the CI (continuous integration) gates that enforce them. The term comes from the [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html), which treats it as a shared commitment rather than a checklist one person holds. Point at the actual CI configuration that enforces it.

### 6. Inclusion Norms

Meeting norms that let everyone contribute (time zones, note-taking, turn-taking), how a teammate requests an accommodation, and how the team surfaces barriers without drama.

### 7. The PR-per-Sprint Norm

Every student owns at least one reviewed pull request per sprint. Record how your team makes that workable (sizing, pairing, review turnaround expectations). This norm feeds the individual contribution modifier on [Sprint Notes](/assignments/sprint-notes/), so write it as something you will actually follow.

### 8. AI and Confidentiality One-Pager

One page stating which data, code, and documents may enter which AI tools, per your partner agreement: what is fully open, what may go only into approved or local tools, and what never leaves the repository. Cover code, partner data, credentials, and meeting content. See the [Generative AI guide](/guides/generative-ai/).

:::caution[NDA teams: this section is load-bearing]
For NDA teams, this one-pager is the difference between normal tool use and a breach. Write it with your partner, name the approved tools explicitly, and treat "we were not sure, so we pasted it anyway" as the incident it is.
:::
