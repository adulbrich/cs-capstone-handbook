Ten weeks in, your team has real patterns: things you do well without noticing and things that hurt every sprint. The retrospective is where you name them while there's still time to act, because winter starts in three weeks with the same people and a harder job.

Fall uses the **4Ls format**: Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for. Run the session as a team (the Retrospectives guide has [the four dimensions](/guides/retrospectives/#4ls) and [how to facilitate](/guides/retrospectives/#running-a-good-retrospective)), then write up what came out of it. Run it under the [retrospective prime directive](https://retrospectivewiki.org/index.php?title=The_Prime_Directive), which assumes everyone did the best they could with what they knew at the time. That's not politeness: a session where people expect blame produces a sanitized list, and a sanitized list gives you nothing to act on.

## What You Must Produce

One document (2 pages of team content plus individual pages), in `docs/retrospectives/fall.md` or as a PDF. NDA teams reference PR titles or IDs only.

Start from the [retrospective template](/team-retrospective-template.md).

### 1. Themes (from the 4Ls)

Distill the session into **2 or 3 themes**: the patterns that actually explain your term, not a list of events. For each theme, give a plausible cause grounded in how the team works (communication, decision-making, workload, review habits), and back it with 1 to 3 lightweight artifacts: a PR link with a one-line caption, a board snapshot, a legible screenshot of a decision thread. If a link is private, include a snippet so graders can evaluate it.

### 2. Team Actions

**Three actions** the team commits to for winter. Each has:

- A single **owner** (a name, not "the team")
- A **timeframe** ("by winter week 3", "starting sprint 1")
- A plain-language **recognition cue** for how you will know it is working ("review turnaround usually same day", "stand-up under ten minutes")

Actions should trace back to your themes. The winter charter refresh (part of the first winter [Repo Checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/)) is where these actions become policy.

### 3. Individual Pages

One short page per student, clearly labeled:

- **1 or 2 wins** and **1 or 2 gaps** from your own term, each tied to a concrete example (a PR, a review, a decision you drove or dodged)
- **One commitment** for winter that is realistic and checkable: what you will do differently, how progress will be visible, and by when

The individual page is graded on wins and gaps tied to concrete examples and one checkable commitment, not on how flattering it is. "I let review requests sit for days; I will clear my review queue before starting new work, visible in review timestamps" beats a paragraph of humble generalities.

The individual-pages criterion is scored per student; a missing or generic individual page costs that student, not the team.

**AI use:** AI may help you structure and phrase the write-up. It cannot attend your retrospective, and it must not invent artifacts or soften findings.

:::note[Winter and spring]
Winter's retrospective is the [Incident Postmortem](/assignments/incident-postmortem/), one concrete failure analyzed. Spring's is the [Project Retrospective](/assignments/project-retrospective/), the cross-year story.
:::
