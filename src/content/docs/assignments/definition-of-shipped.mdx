Your spring grade's largest partner-evaluation facet (Verification and Validation, 40%) is scored against **outcomes**: is the thing in production, merged upstream, reproducible, in real use? Those bars are hard. This document is how you stop them from being a surprise: a short, explicit agreement with your project partner about what "shipped" means for **this** project, written while there is still time to act on it.

With AI tools, building is rarely the long pole anymore. **The external world is**: maintainer review latency, app store review, IRB and data access, partner IT security review, real-user recruitment. Every one of those clocks is measured in weeks. This document plots them on the calendar.

## What It Must Contain (1 to 2 pages, in `docs/shipped.md`)

1. **The target rung**: your [outcome type](/practicalities/types/), and which rung of the [outcome ladder](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/#spring) you are committing to, in your own words, specific to your project.
2. **Success metrics**: 2 or 3 measurable signals of "it worked" (users completing a task, benchmark result, uptime, merged PRs), and how at least one will be instrumented for real before the final release.
3. **Prerequisites and lead times**: everything the target rung requires that your team does not control (accounts, reviews, approvals, recruitment), each with an owner and a start-by date. See the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) for your outcome type's known lead-time bombs.
4. **User access plan**: who the real users or evaluators are and how you will reach them by the winter real-user gate.
5. **Partner agreement**: evidence the partner has seen and agreed to this definition (meeting note, email, or survey confirmation).

Start from the [Definition of Shipped template](/definition-of-shipped-template.md).

:::note[Unresponsive partner]
A non-responsive partner does not tank your grade. If the partner has not engaged after two documented attempts (email plus your regular meeting channel), tell your TA: your mentor or an instructor reviews and signs the definition instead, and the partner-agreement criterion is scored on your documented attempts.
:::

## Rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Target rung explicit, project-specific, and honest about ambition | 25 | SO2 |
| Metrics measurable, with a concrete instrumentation plan for at least one | 25 | SO2 |
| Prerequisites and lead times complete, owned, and dated (nothing discoverable in week 8 of spring should be missing) | 25 | SO1 |
| Partner agreement evidenced | 15 | SO3 |
| Writing: brief, unambiguous, usable as a checklist | 10 | SO3 |

**AI use:** AI may draft the document from your Lean Canvas, your requirements, and the Shipping guide's lead-time tables. It must not invent the partner agreement, a named user, or a lead time nobody checked. A fabricated partner confirmation or a made-up start-by date fails the assignment, because this document is a commitment other people will hold you to.

:::caution[Renegotiation is allowed; surprise is not]
Projects change. If the target rung becomes unreachable for reasons you can document, renegotiate with your partner and update `docs/shipped.md` (git history is the record). Spring partner evaluations go badly when the partner and the team discover in spring week 10 that they had different definitions all along.
:::
