Your spring grade's largest partner-evaluation facet (Verification and Validation, 40%) is scored against **outcomes**: is the thing in production, merged upstream, reproducible, in real use? Those bars are hard. This document is how you stop them from being a surprise: a short, explicit agreement with your project partner about what "shipped" means for **this** project, written while there is still time to act on it.

With AI tools, building is rarely the long pole anymore. **The external world is**: maintainer review latency, app store review, [IRB](https://research.oregonstate.edu/ori/irb) (Institutional Review Board) and data access, partner IT security review, real-user recruitment. Every one of those clocks is measured in weeks. This document plots them on the calendar.

## What It Must Contain (1 to 2 pages, in `docs/shipped.md`)

1. **The expectations you agreed**: what shipped means for this project, in your own words, and which rung of the [outcome ladder](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/#spring) you are committing to.

   You and your project partner or mentor agree this together, and this document is where you record it. Nobody assigns it to you. The [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) works through four paths other teams have taken, FOSS, Research, Consultancy and New Product or Game, with the rungs and the lead times each one involves; read the one closest to your project. If none of them describes yours, write your own expectations here instead. That is fully fine and costs you nothing, because the rubrics read what you committed to, not which example you resemble. Nothing is settled when a project is submitted, because no team exists yet and the submission portal's topic tag is a different thing.
2. **Success metrics**: 2 or 3 measurable signals of "it worked" (users completing a task, benchmark result, uptime, merged PRs), and how at least one will be instrumented for real before the final release.
3. **Prerequisites and lead times**: everything the target rung requires that your team does not control (accounts, reviews, approvals, recruitment), each with an owner and a start-by date. See the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) for the lead-time bombs each path involves.
4. **User access plan**: who the real users or evaluators are and how you will reach them by the winter real-user gate.
5. **Partner agreement**: evidence the partner has seen and agreed to this definition (meeting note, email, or survey confirmation).
6. **If other teams work on this project**: what your team owns, agreed with your project partner rather than assumed. Which interface, repository, or milestone is yours, and how the teams talk to each other. Skip this item if your team is the only one on the project. It is scored under the target rung, because a rung you cannot reach without another team delivering its half is not a rung you have committed to.

Start from the [Definition of Shipped template](/definition-of-shipped-template.md).

:::note[Unresponsive partner]
A non-responsive partner does not tank your grade. If the partner has not engaged after two documented attempts (email plus your regular meeting channel), tell your Teaching Assistant (TA): your mentor or an instructor reviews and signs the definition instead, and the partner-agreement criterion is scored on your documented attempts.
:::

## Rubric (100 points)

<RubricTable tsv={rubricTsv} sourceLabel="canvas/assignments/definition-of-shipped/definition-of-shipped-rubric-details.tsv" />

**AI use:** AI may draft the document from your [Lean Canvas](/activities/requirements/#lean-canvas), your requirements, and the Shipping guide's lead-time tables. It must not invent the partner agreement, a named user, or a lead time nobody checked. A fabricated partner confirmation or a made-up start-by date fails the assignment, because this document is a commitment other people will hold you to.

:::caution[Renegotiation is allowed; surprise is not]
Projects change. If the target rung becomes unreachable for reasons you can document, renegotiate with your partner and update `docs/shipped.md` (git history is the record). Spring partner evaluations go badly when the partner and the team discover in spring week 10 that they had different definitions all along.
:::
