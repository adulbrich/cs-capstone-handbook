Your spring grade's largest partner-evaluation facet (Verification and Validation, 40%) is scored against outcomes: is the thing in production, merged upstream, reproducible, in real use? This document keeps those bars from being a surprise: a short, explicit agreement with your project partner about what "shipped" means for this project, written while there's still time to act on it.

Your target rung depends on clocks your team doesn't control: maintainer review latency, app store review, [IRB](https://research.oregonstate.edu/ori/irb) (Institutional Review Board) approval and data access, partner IT security review, and real-user recruitment. Each one runs in weeks, and this document gives each one an owner and a start-by date.

## What It Must Contain (1 to 2 pages)

The document is your agreement with your partner, kept with [your team's documents](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/#what-the-repository-must-contain-the-living-docs), not as a file in the repository. What the code needs from it goes where your AI tools and a successor will find it: the success metrics and how each is measured in `docs/requirements.md` or the AI context file, a decision it forces in an ADR, and the instrumentation in the code.

1. **The expectations you agreed**: what shipped means for this project, in your own words, and which rung of the [outcome ladder](/assignments/project-partner-evaluation/#spring) you are committing to.

   You and your project partner or mentor agree this together, and this document is where you record it. Nobody assigns it to you. The [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) works through four example paths (FOSS, Research, Consultancy, and New Product or Game) with the rungs and the lead times each one involves; read the one closest to your project. If none of them describes yours, write your own expectations here instead. That costs you nothing, because the rubrics read what you committed to, not which example you resemble. Nothing is settled when a project is submitted, because no team exists yet and the submission portal's topic tag is a different thing.
2. **Success metrics**: 2 or 3 measurable signals of "it worked" (users completing a task, benchmark result, uptime, merged PRs), and how at least one will be instrumented for real before the final release.
3. **Prerequisites and lead times**: everything the target rung requires that your team does not control (accounts, reviews, approvals, recruitment), each with an owner and a start-by date. See the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) for the lead-time bombs each path involves.
4. **User access plan**: who the real users or evaluators are and how you will reach them by the winter real-user gate.
5. **Partner agreement**: evidence the partner has seen and agreed to this definition (meeting note, email, or survey confirmation).
6. **If other teams work on this project**: what your team owns, agreed with your project partner rather than assumed. Which interface, repository, or milestone is yours, and how the teams talk to each other. Skip this item if your team is the only one on the project. It is scored under the target rung, because a rung you cannot reach without another team delivering its half is not a rung you have committed to.

Start from the [Definition of Shipped template](/definition-of-shipped-template.md).

:::note[Unresponsive partner]
A non-responsive partner does not tank your grade. If the partner has not engaged after two documented attempts (email plus your regular meeting channel), tell your Teaching Assistant (TA): your mentor or an instructor reviews and signs the definition instead, and the partner-agreement criterion is scored on your documented attempts.
:::

**AI use:** AI may draft the document from your [Lean Canvas](/activities/requirements/#lean-canvas), your requirements, and the Shipping guide's lead-time tables. It must not invent the partner agreement, a named user, or a lead time nobody checked. A fabricated partner confirmation or a made-up start-by date fails the assignment, because this document is a commitment other people will hold you to.

:::caution[Renegotiation is allowed; surprise is not]
Projects change. If the target rung becomes unreachable for reasons you can document, renegotiate with your partner, record the new version and the partner's agreement to it, and update the metrics in the repository to match. Spring partner evaluations go badly when the partner and the team discover in spring week 10 that they had different definitions all along.
:::
