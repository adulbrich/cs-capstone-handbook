**Team assessment. Fall and winter, twice per term (4% each), around weeks 5 and 10.**

There is no requirements document assignment and no technical design document assignment in this course. Those documents exist, but they live in your repository under `docs/`, they stay current because your team (and your AI tools) need them current, and we grade them **in place**. A checkpoint is a staff review of the repository as it actually is: no export, no upload, no drift between the document and the code.

## What the Repository Must Contain (the living docs)

- `docs/requirements.md`: what the product must do and how you will know, with acceptance criteria for current priorities. Updated as scope changes; git history is the change log.
- `docs/design.md`: the system view someone would need to contribute safely: context, key diagrams, interfaces and contracts, deployment shape.
- `docs/rfcs/`: RFCs and recorded decisions (see [RFC](/assignments/rfc/) and the [ADR guide](/guides/adr/)).
- `docs/risks.md`: **one** risk register for the whole project (process, product, and technical risks together), ranked, with owners. This is the only risk register; do not maintain copies elsewhere.
- `README.md`: an honest quickstart that a new contributor (or grader) can actually follow.
- `CONTRIBUTING.md` and the AI context file (`CLAUDE.md`, `.github/copilot-instructions.md`, or equivalent) from your [Team Charter](/assignments/team-charter/).

## Term Gates

Each term's second checkpoint carries a gate that pulls the year's risk forward:

| Checkpoint | Gate |
|---|---|
| Fall, week 5 | Rails stood up: repo, CI, board, living docs exist and are real |
| Fall, week 10 | **"Hello, production"**: a [walking skeleton](/walking-skeleton-prototype-template.md) (the thinnest end-to-end slice of your system that actually runs) deployed to a production-like environment (or: first upstream PR submitted for FOSS; pipeline runs end to end on sample data for research), plus a Definition of Shipped v0 draft |
| Winter, week 5 | Integration health: the skeleton has organs; CI meaningful; real-user session scheduled |
| Winter, week 10 | **Release candidate**: installable/deployable by a stranger, plus evidence of at least one real-user session |

The gates exist because the spring outcome bar is won or lost on lead times that start now. See the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) for your category's path.

## Rubric (100 points per checkpoint)

| Criterion | Points | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Living docs current and consistent with the code (spot-checked: does `docs/design.md` describe the system that exists?) | 25 | SO2 |
| Term gate met (see table above) | 25 | SO2 |
| Build health and safety net: CI green, tests exist and test something real, quickstart works, and the net matches how much the team delegates (review gates on the paths that are hard to undo, a staging or preview environment, a documented rollback path) | 20 | SO6 |
| Decision and risk hygiene: RFCs/ADRs recorded, single risk register alive and ranked | 15 | SO1 |
| Contribution traceability: the infrastructure exists to attribute work per student (PRs authored and reviewed under real accounts, contribution lines in sprint notes match the record) | 15 | SO5 |

## Track A and Track B

- **Track A (staff repo access, the default):** graded asynchronously; staff may leave issues or PR comments as feedback.
- **Track B (NDA):** the checkpoint is a 15-minute screen-shared walkthrough in your cohort check-in. A designated student drives; the TA follows the same checklist against the live repo and retains nothing. Where the partner permits, a sanitized evidence pack (docs snapshot, redacted PR list, CI screenshots) supports the review. Identical criteria, identical points.

## How Graders Review

To keep graders consistent: "tests something real" means at least one test would fail if the feature it covers broke (per category: user-visible behavior for products, pipeline correctness for research, upstream test conventions for FOSS); "quickstart works" means a grader followed it cold; the docs spot-check compares one recent feature against `docs/design.md`. The contribution-traceability criterion grades whether the **infrastructure** for attribution exists, not whether every student contributed: individual non-contribution is handled by the individual modifier, never double-counted here. Track B walkthroughs follow a fixed order (quickstart, CI, docs spot-check, risk register, PR list) so 15 minutes suffices.

The **safety net** half of build health is judged relative to how the team works, not against a fixed tool list. A team that delegates heavily to AI tools needs tighter automated gates than a team that writes most code by hand, and neither approach is penalized: what is graded is whether the net actually catches what the team's way of working lets through. Look for gates on the changes that are expensive to undo (schema, authentication, deployment, migrations) rather than uniform coverage everywhere. A team with no CI that hand-merges agent output to production fails this half regardless of whether anything has broken yet.

:::note[Why this replaces document assignments]
A submitted PDF is a snapshot that starts rotting at upload time. A repository is either healthy or it is not, and your project partner, your future maintainers, and your AI tools all consume the repository, not the PDF. Grading the real thing aligns the course with how you actually work.
:::

## Activities That Prepare This

Checkpoints happen four times across fall and winter, and each gate needs different preparation, so the recommendations are grouped by gate rather than offered as one list.

**Fall week 5, rails stood up:** [Describe your Architecture](/activities/design/#describe-your-architecture-️) and [User Story Mapping](/activities/requirements/#user-story-mapping-️) for the living docs; [Kanban Board Setup](/activities/planning/#kanban-board-setup) and [Definition of Done](/activities/planning/#definition-of-done) once, for the gate and build health; [Audit Your Safety Net](/activities/ai/#audit-your-safety-net) for the safety-net half.

**Fall week 10, "hello, production":** [Deployment Plan](/activities/design/#deployment-plan), [Plan Prototype](/activities/planning/#plan-prototype), and [Software Release and Versioning](/activities/planning/#software-release-and-versioning) for the gate; [Put a Browser Agent on Your Critical Flow](/activities/ai/#put-a-browser-agent-on-your-critical-flow) for build health.

**Winter week 5, integration health:** [Test Plan](/activities/design/#test-plan) and [Wire an Accessibility Audit Into CI](/activities/ai/#wire-an-accessibility-audit-into-ci) for build health; [Find Users](/activities/user/#find-users) then [Engage Users](/activities/user/#engage-users) for the scheduled real-user session; [Risk Management Plan](/activities/planning/#risk-management-plan) for risk hygiene.

**Winter week 10, release candidate:** [Test with an Assistive Technology User](/activities/user/#test-with-an-assistive-technology-user) and [Run an Acceptance Pass](/activities/ai/#run-an-acceptance-pass) for the real-user evidence; [Describe your API Reference](/activities/design/#describe-your-api-reference) for the living docs; [Fishbone Diagram](/activities/planning/#fishbone-diagram) for risk hygiene.

Browse [design](/activities/design/) and [planning](/activities/planning/) when these run out.
