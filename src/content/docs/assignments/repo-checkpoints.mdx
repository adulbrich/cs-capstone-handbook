<AssignmentSummary />

Four times a year, in weeks 5 and 10 of fall and winter, the instruction team reviews your repository at a commit you name, and may leave feedback in it as issues or pull request comments. You submit one PDF of the repository's living docs, the seven documents listed below, at that commit, with a cover that names the commit and links your milestone's evidence. Graders check [continuous integration](https://martinfowler.com/articles/continuousIntegration.html) (CI), the history, and the milestone in the repository itself, so the documents must match the code.

## What You Produce

You produce the repository. The four subsections below are the criteria the rubric applies at every checkpoint, under the same names; then comes the checkpoint's milestone, which the Milestone met criterion scores. The repository holds what an AI tool or a successor needs to work on the product, and nothing about the team: team documents (the charter, the retrospectives, the Definition of Shipped, and any postmortem on a non-technical incident) live where your team keeps its own files, and the [handoff document](/assignments/project-handoff/) where your partner keeps project documents.

**AI use:** AI may draft and maintain the living docs from your code and history, and may write the CI configuration; the checkpoint grades the repository, not who typed it. What fails the checkpoint is evidence that is not real: a green badge from a workflow that runs nothing, a requirements document describing features the code does not have, or a pull request history dressed up after the fact.

### Docs Current and Consistent With the Code

Five of the seven living docs, each describing the code at the named commit:

- **`docs/requirements.md`**: the problem statement; functional and non-functional requirements, with an acceptance criterion wherever the behavior isn't obvious (a finished functional requirement can name its test in CI instead); the limits you did not choose, under Constraints; success metrics; non-goals; open questions. *Guide: [Requirements](/guides/requirements/).*
- **`docs/design.md`**: the system as it exists, with its context, key diagrams, interfaces and contracts, the data model, the deployment shape, decisions deferred on purpose, and open technical questions. *Guide: [Technical design](/guides/technical-design/).*
- **`README.md`**: a quickstart from a clean clone to a running system. *Reference: [makeareadme.com](https://www.makeareadme.com/).*
- **`CONTRIBUTING.md`**: the sections [the charter requires](/assignments/team-charter/#required-in-contributingmd). *Reference: [GitHub's guidelines](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/setting-up-your-project-for-healthy-contributions/setting-guidelines-for-repository-contributors).*
- **AI context file**: `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, or your tool's equivalent, pointing at these documents rather than copying them. If you keep several, the others point at `AGENTS.md`, since two instruction files that disagree are worse than one. *Reference: [agents.md](https://agents.md/).*

Graders compare one recent feature with `docs/design.md` and one document in the PDF with the same file at the commit, and read the Constraints section of `docs/requirements.md`.

### Decision and Risk Hygiene

The other two living docs:

- **`docs/rfcs/` or `docs/adr/`**: one record per decision, as a request for comments (RFC) or an architecture decision record (ADR), with its outcome and what was rejected and why, linked from `docs/design.md` where it applies. *Guide: [ADR](/guides/adr/).*
- **`docs/risks.md`**: the one risk register, covering business, technical, and team risks, ranked, each with an owner, plus every external prerequisite (an approval, an account, a review, recruitment) with who asked for it and when, updated between checkpoints. Write each risk as a fact about the project, without blame; a problem between teammates goes to your Teaching Assistant (TA). *Guide: [Project Risks](/guides/risks/).*

Graders check first that `docs/risks.md` is the only register and that every entry in it has a rank and an owner.

### Build Health and Safety Net

CI runs your tests on every pull request and passed on the named commit, and at least one test fails when the behavior it covers breaks. Break something on a branch once and watch CI go red, so you know it can.

| If your project is | CI is |
|---|---|
| New Product or Game, Consultancy | A workflow that builds the project and runs the tests on every pull request |
| Free and open-source (FOSS) | Upstream's CI on your pull requests. Until your first upstream pull request, the build-and-test command upstream expects, documented in your fork and passing on the named commit |
| Research | The pipeline run end to end on a small sample dataset, with at least one test asserting a known result rather than merely exiting zero |

The safety net is review gates on changes that are expensive to undo (schema, authentication, deployment, migrations), somewhere to exercise changes before they reach users (a staging or preview environment, a store beta track, a test device, a run on sample data), and a documented rollback path, sized to how your team works: the more you delegate to AI tools, the more the automated checks must catch before a change merges.

The `README.md` quickstart works from a clean clone: graders follow it first, so follow it yourself before you submit.

### Contribution Traceability and Version Control Hygiene

Nothing reaches the default branch except through a reviewed pull request, and the history meets the five items of [a history a maintainer could inherit](/guides/git-and-github/#a-history-a-maintainer-could-inherit). Each student's code arrives through pull requests under their own account, other work sits where `CONTRIBUTING.md` says it lives, and the Contribution lines in your [sprint notes](/assignments/sprint-notes/) match that record. Graders check whether each student's work can be attributed; how much each did is graded in your [individual contribution](/assignments/sprint-notes/#sprint-notes-n-individual-contribution).

### Repo Checkpoint 1: Set Up

Due fall week 5. Milestone met scores each item below, and Submission complete scores the PDF itself. Agree the production target now: an account, a hosting decision, or a review can take weeks.

- **CI**: passed on the named commit, in [your project's form of CI](#build-health-and-safety-net). With no product code yet, it runs on a scaffold: your stack's starter project, with at least one test that fails when the scaffold breaks. *Evidence: a link on the cover to the run.*
- **Board**: every item in the current sprint has an owner and a status. *Evidence: a link on the cover to the board.*
- **Production target**: one paragraph says where the [walking skeleton](https://wiki.c2.com/?WalkingSkeleton) (the thinnest end-to-end slice of your system that works) will run, as [What Deployed Means in Fall](#what-deployed-means-in-fall) defines it for your project; who owns the account or infrastructure; and who agreed it, your partner or, without one, your mentor. FOSS projects add who upstream reviews the first pull request; research projects, which sample data the pipeline may use and, if the real-data run will happen on a single workstation, who outside the team will run it there. With no deployment planned, the paragraph says why and what ships instead, so the instruction team can agree an equivalent milestone with you. *Evidence: the deployment section of `docs/design.md`.*
- **Approval**: if the target needs one, an entry names it, who is asking for it, and when they asked. *Evidence: an entry in `docs/risks.md`.*
- **Inherited-codebase audit**: if you start in an existing codebase, your audit covers everything listed under [The Inherited-Codebase Audit](#the-inherited-codebase-audit). *Evidence: a document in the PDF, after the living docs.*

Two criteria ask for less at this checkpoint. Under Decision and Risk Hygiene, an [RFC](/assignments/rfc/) draft committed with status Proposed, naming the options it weighs, counts as a recorded decision. Under Build Health and Safety Net, staging and rollback can be a plan in `docs/risks.md` or `README.md`, saying where changes will be exercised before they reach users and how a bad change will be undone, each with the date it lands; and with no product code yet, the `README.md` quickstart may start only the scaffold.

#### The Inherited-Codebase Audit

If you start in an existing codebase (a predecessor team's, your partner's, or an upstream project's), its decisions, tests, debt, and review process are yours before you write a line. Audit it in fall weeks 1 to 5, covering:

- what runs and how, and what does not;
- the state of the tests and the CI;
- the dependencies and their age;
- the open risks and the known debt;
- the predecessor team's handoff document and the forward-looking section of their project retrospective, if there is one;
- the upstream project's contribution rules, if there are any;
- what you will change first.

Put what the audit finds that the code depends on where the next person looks: constraints in `docs/requirements.md`; conventions and pitfalls in whichever living doc covers them, such as `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `docs/design.md`, or the AI context file; and a decision to keep or replace something in an ADR.

### Repo Checkpoint 2: Hello, Production

Due fall week 10. Milestone met scores each item below.

- **Walking skeleton deployed**: the [walking skeleton](https://wiki.c2.com/?WalkingSkeleton) (the thinnest end-to-end slice of your system that works) is deployed from the named commit to the target `docs/design.md` recorded at Repo Checkpoint 1, or one agreed since and recorded there, as [What Deployed Means in Fall](#what-deployed-means-in-fall) defines deployed for your project. *Evidence: a link on the cover to what that section names for your project.*
- **Definition of Shipped v0**: a draft of every item the [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) asks for, whether or not your partner has agreed it yet. *Evidence: a document in the PDF, after the living docs.*

### Repo Checkpoint 3: Integration Health

Due winter week 5. Milestone met scores each item below.

- **Integration**: every story your board shows as done runs end to end through your real system, with no part of your own code replaced by a stub (a placeholder that returns canned answers). *Evidence: the board, linked on the cover, where each done story says how to run it: its steps or its test.*
- **CI**: at least one test in the CI run on the named commit covers one of those stories. *Evidence: a link on the cover to the run.*
- **Real-user session scheduled**: an entry gives the session's date, the participant's role (not their name), and who confirmed it. *Evidence: an external prerequisite in `docs/risks.md`.*

### Repo Checkpoint 4: Release Candidate

Due winter week 10. Milestone met scores each item below.

- **Release candidate**: a release built from the named commit (a tagged version, a store build, or a release page) installs or deploys for someone outside the team, a grader included, using only `README.md`. *Evidence: a link on the cover to the release.*
- **Real-user session**: notes from at least one session, giving the participant's role (not their name), what they tried, what went wrong or confused them, and the issue or change each finding produced. *Evidence: a document in the PDF, after the living docs.*

## What Deployed Means in Fall

Repo Checkpoint 1 records where your walking skeleton will run in week 10, and Repo Checkpoint 2 checks that it does. Deployed means one of the five options below; your path in the [Shipping guide](/guides/shipping/) and what you build decide which:

- **A web app or hosted service** (New Product or Game, Consultancy): the skeleton serves requests in the production target. *Evidence: a link on the cover to its URL.*
- **A mobile app, game, desktop application, or firmware** (New Product or Game, Consultancy): a build installs from the channel `docs/design.md` names (a store test track, a release page, a device image) on a device outside the team. *Evidence: a link on the cover to the build's page on that channel.*
- **FOSS**: your first upstream pull request is submitted. *Evidence: a link on the cover to the pull request.*
- **Research**: the pipeline runs end to end on sample data in the compute environment your real-data run will use, such as your partner's cluster, a university high-performance computing cluster, or a cloud allocation. If the real-data run will happen on a single workstation, someone outside the team, such as your partner or a member of their group, runs it there and reports the run. *Evidence: a link on the cover to the job's log or output, or to the issue in your repository where that person reports the run.*
- **No deployment, by agreement** (any project): the equivalent milestone the instruction team agreed with you at Repo Checkpoint 1. *Evidence: the cover names that milestone and links its evidence.*

## What You Submit

One PDF. Its cover is the one [Submitting Your Work](/assignments/introduction/#submitting-your-work) describes, plus the repository URL, the full hash of the commit you want graded, the links your milestone names, and, for any milestone item not yet met, who owns it and the date it will be met. Then come the seven living docs at that commit, rendered, in this order: `docs/requirements.md`, `docs/design.md`, each record in `docs/rfcs/` or `docs/adr/`, `docs/risks.md`, `README.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, and the AI context file; then any document your milestone names. Push that commit before you submit, so graders can reach it.

:::note[If your project is under NDA]
If your partner rules out read access for the instruction team, the checkpoint is a 15-minute screen-shared walkthrough of the repository at the named commit, in a slot your team and your TA schedule in the checkpoint week, separate from your sprint demo, graded on the same criteria. Submit the PDF [sanitized](/introduction/ip-nda-and-licensing/#teams-under-an-nda) where your partner permits, or the cover alone where it permits no copy. In the walkthrough the TA opens each link on your cover live, so a link a grader cannot open does not fail the milestone.
:::

## Activities That Prepare This

**Repo Checkpoint 2: Hello, Production, fall week 10:** [Deployment Plan](/activities/testing-release-and-handoff/#deployment-plan), [Plan Prototype](/activities/planning-and-risk/#plan-prototype), and [Software Release and Versioning](/activities/testing-release-and-handoff/#software-release-and-versioning) for the milestone; [Put a Browser Agent on Your Critical Flow](/activities/testing-release-and-handoff/#put-a-browser-agent-on-your-critical-flow) for the safety net if your product runs in a browser.
