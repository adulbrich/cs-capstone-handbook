Nine activities from the [practice library](/activities/introduction/) are run as workshops in class: four in fall, three in winter, and two in spring. Each session spends its first half on the activity and its second half redoing it with an AI agent, so the AI half is part of the workshop, never a separate item. They are graded, but only on whether you did them: there is no quality bar, no bands, and no rubric. Submit the output and you get the points.

They are graded at all for one reason: each one makes several later rubric criteria substantially easier, and every year some teams skip the cheap preparation and pay for it in week 8. The points are small enough that they are not worth optimizing and large enough that skipping them is a visible choice.

## Fall (4 items, 2%)

| Activity | Week | What it makes easier |
|---|---|---|
| [Team Formation Strategies Assessment](/activities/teamwork/#team-formation-strategies-assessment) | 1 | The [Team Charter](/assignments/team-charter/) conflict and inclusion section, written the week you meet; the defense role question |
| [User Story Mapping](/activities/requirements/#user-story-mapping) | 2 | `docs/requirements.md` at every [repo checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/); the winter [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) |
| [Describe your Architecture](/activities/design/#describe-your-architecture) | 3 | `docs/design.md`, your [RFC](/assignments/rfc/), and the [defense](/assignments/defense/) technical-depth question |
| [Audit Your Safety Net](/activities/ai/#audit-your-safety-net) | 3 | Four criteria across the checkpoints, the RFC, and the defense |

Week 3 is one session with two outputs: the architecture and the safety net that guards it. Both land before the RFC draft at the end of week 4. [Map Your One-Way Doors](/activities/ai/#map-your-one-way-doors) is no longer a workshop but still picks your RFC topic; do it in week 3 on your own, it takes an hour.

## Winter (3 items, 1%)

| Activity | Week | What it makes easier |
|---|---|---|
| [Dependency Mapping or Critical Path Analysis](/activities/planning/#dependency-mapping-or-critical-path-analysis) | 1 | The [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/), due two weeks later: the external clocks named and started |
| [Team Dysfunctions Assessment](/activities/teamwork/#team-dysfunctions-assessment) | 2 | The midterm [peer evaluation](/assignments/peer-evaluations/) in week 5: naming a problem before the survey does; the [defense](/assignments/defense/) role question later |
| [Test Plan](/activities/design/#test-plan) | 3 | The week-5 [repo checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/): the tests in CI that must exist before real users arrive at the release candidate |

## Spring (2 items, 1%)

| Activity | Week | What it makes easier |
|---|---|---|
| [Team Health Assessment](/activities/teamwork/#team-health-assessment) | 2 | The spring [charter](/assignments/team-charter/) refresh and the midterm [peer evaluation](/assignments/peer-evaluations/); the [Project Retrospective](/assignments/project-retrospective/) team-dynamics criterion later |
| [Identify Success Metrics](/activities/planning/#identify-success-metrics) | 3 | [Release and Metrics](/assignments/release/) and the [Landing Page](/assignments/landing-page/): the number that proves it worked, chosen while there is time to instrument it |

The spring assessment closes with the charter refresh: update `docs/charter.md` with what it surfaced (roles for the final term, the decision process, anything the winter postmortem changed) and commit it. Spring has no repo checkpoint to carry the refresh, so this workshop is where it lives.

The teamwork slot rotates through three different instruments so it tells you something new each term rather than asking you to retake the same survey.

## Grading

One Canvas column per term, scored as complete/incomplete per item: 4 checkboxes in fall, 3 in winter, 2 in spring. The spring week-6 Resume Building session is a workshop too, but its output is graded inside the [Career and Individual Retrospective](/assignments/career-retrospective/), not here. Submit the activity's output, whatever the activity says a good output is. A grader spends seconds per item confirming something real was submitted.

There is no partial credit within an item and no quality judgment. An output that is obviously not the activity (a blank file, an unrelated document) does not count, but a genuine attempt always does.

**Late work:** these are workshop activities tied to a week, so the standard [late policy](/assignments/introduction/#late-work-absence-and-makeup) applies with one relaxation: a workshop item submitted any time before the end of the term still earns its points. Doing it late costs you the benefit, not the mark.

**Missed the workshop session?** The activity pages are public and self-contained. Read the page, do the activity, submit it.

:::note[NDA teams]
Every workshop activity produces output that stays inside your repository or your team's own documents. Submit a sanitized note confirming what you did, or tell your TA and cover it in your next check-in. No partner-confidential material needs to leave your boundary for these.
:::

:::tip[Why these nine and not others]
Each one prepares an assignment due within three weeks of it and heads off a failure the course has seen: teams that never mapped scope, never named their external clocks, never wrote the tests before the users came. Each works for every project category and takes about an hour. The more than a hundred other activities in the library are genuinely optional; these nine are the ones that pay off more than once.
:::
