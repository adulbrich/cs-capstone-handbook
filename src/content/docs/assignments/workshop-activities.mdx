Nine activities from the [practice library](/activities/introduction/) are run as workshops in class: five in fall, three in winter, and one in spring. Each session runs the activity twice: once by hand, then once with an AI agent. Both outputs go in the same submission. They are graded, but only on whether you did them: there is no quality bar. Submit the output and you get the points.

Each one makes several later rubric criteria easier; the tables below name which, together with the Canvas entry each one is and the output a complete submission carries (the activity's own "A good output" line). The more than a hundred other activities in the [library](/activities/introduction/) are optional.

## Fall (5 items, 2%)

| Canvas entry | Week | A complete submission is the activity's good output | What it makes easier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workshop 1: [User Story Mapping](/activities/requirements/#user-story-mapping) | 2 | A user story map with epics and written user stories, each marked in or out of scope for this year | `docs/requirements.md` at every [repo checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/), in the shape the [requirements guide](/guides/requirements/#story-mapping) sets out; the winter [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) |
| Workshop 2: [Map Your Hard-to-Reverse Decisions](/activities/ai/#map-your-hard-to-reverse-decisions) | 3 | The list of upcoming decisions with the few hard-to-reverse ones marked | Your [RFC](/assignments/rfc/) topic, which has to be a decision that is hard to reverse; the [defense](/assignments/defense/) judgment question |
| Workshop 3: [Audit Your Safety Net](/activities/ai/#audit-your-safety-net) | 3 | One specific named gap in your checks and the smallest change that closes it | Four criteria across the checkpoints, the RFC, and the defense |
| Workshop 4: [Peer Technical Design Review](/activities/design/#peer-technical-design-review) | 7 | Your design packet, the review you wrote of each of the other two packets, and a note on what you changed in your design after reading theirs | The [RFC](/assignments/rfc/) final, due the week after: its revision criterion, and the [defense](/assignments/defense/) technical-depth question |
| Workshop 5: [Identify Success Metrics](/activities/planning/#identify-success-metrics) | 8 | 2 or 3 metrics with their decision rules and a named instrumentation method for at least one, committed and dated | The v0 [Definition of Shipped](/assignments/definition-of-shipped/) draft at the week 10 [repo checkpoint](/assignments/repo-checkpoints/), and the spring [Release and Metrics](/assignments/release/): the number that proves it worked, chosen while there is still time to instrument it |

The week 3 session runs two activities, Workshop 2 and Workshop 3, and each is its own Canvas entry with its own submission. Both land before the RFC draft at the end of week 4, and the hard-to-reverse decision you pick in Workshop 2 is usually the RFC topic. [Describe your Architecture](/activities/design/#describe-your-architecture) is recommended the same week and takes an hour or two on your own; it feeds `docs/design.md` and the RFC framing.

Workshop 4 runs asynchronously, in triads of teams the instruction team publishes in Canvas at the start of week 6. Post your team's design packet (the one-page diagram and one page of text the activity describes) in your triad's Canvas discussion by the end of week 6, and post your reviews of the other two packets there before the week 7 class session. The session is working time: read the reviews you received and decide what changes in your design. The Workshop 4 Canvas entry then takes one PDF holding the packet, your two reviews, and the note on what you changed.

## Spring (1 item, 1%)

| Canvas entry | Week | A complete submission is the activity's good output | What it makes easier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workshop 1: [Team Health Assessment](/activities/teamwork/#team-health-assessment) | 2 | Your completed health check with its two lowest dimensions carried into your next retrospective, plus the charter refresh below | The spring [charter](/assignments/team-charter/) refresh and the midterm [peer evaluation](/assignments/peer-evaluations/); the [Project Retrospective](/assignments/project-retrospective/) team-dynamics criterion later |

The spring assessment closes with the charter refresh: update `docs/charter.md` (the [working agreement guide](/guides/working-agreement/) has the patterns) with what it surfaced (roles for the final term, the decision process, anything the winter postmortem changed) and commit it. Spring has no repo checkpoint to carry the refresh, so this workshop is where it lives.

## Rubric (100 points per workshop)

Each workshop is its own Canvas entry, scored with this rubric; the tables above say what a complete submission shows for each. The spring week-6 Resume Building session runs like a workshop but is not scored here.

<RubricTable tsv={rubricTsv} sourceLabel="canvas/assignments/workshop-activities/workshop-activities-rubric-details.tsv" />

Submit the activity's output as one PDF, whatever the activity says a good output is; a photo of a whiteboard or sticky notes goes inside the PDF. A grader spends seconds per item confirming something real was submitted.

There is no partial credit within an item and no quality judgment. An output that is obviously not the activity (a blank file, an unrelated document) does not count, but a genuine attempt always does.

**Late work:** these are workshop activities tied to a week, so the standard [late policy](/assignments/introduction/#late-work-absence-and-makeup) applies with one relaxation: a workshop item submitted any time before the end of the term still earns its points.

Missed the workshop session? The activity pages are public and self-contained, so do the activity from its page and submit the output as usual.

:::note[NDA teams]
Every workshop except Workshop 4 produces output that stays inside your repository or your team's own documents: submit a sanitized PDF note confirming what you did, or tell your Teaching Assistant (TA) and cover it in your next check-in. In Workshop 4 the other teams in your triad read your packet, so write it as a decision skeleton the way the [RFC's NDA note](/assignments/rfc/#the-cycle) describes: the structure and the reasoning, with neutral placeholders for confidential specifics. They cannot open your repository, so say so in the packet and they review the design without the code check.
:::
