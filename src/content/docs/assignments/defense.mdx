**Individual assessment, held as a team session. Every term. 10% of the term grade.**

Once per term, part of a cohort check-in (weeks 7 to 9) becomes a defense: 30 to 40 minutes for the whole team, during which **each student** walks through something they own and answers live questions. In spring, strong individual performance at the Expo Q&A can substitute, at the instructors' discretion.

You may present a pull request you drove, a subsystem you maintain, your RFC decision, or a test or deployment strategy you built. The questions are the point: why this way, what alternatives did you reject, what breaks if X changes, what did the AI get wrong along the way and how did you catch it.

**Ground rules:** your docs, your repo, and your AI tools are open **for explaining** (looking things up, walking through code) and closed **for generating** (no asking a model to produce the answer live). Notes are fine. Slides are unnecessary.

This is deliberately the assessment AI cannot do for you. If you built and understood your work, the defense is a friendly conversation about it. If you cannot explain your own contribution, no artifact will cover for that, and that is by design: your project partner will expect the same of you in every meeting.

## Scoring

TAs and instructors score each student live on a printed sheet. No written deliverable exists; there is nothing to prepare beyond doing your work and being able to talk about it.

## Rubric (100 points, per student)

| Criterion | Points | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership: presents work they demonstrably own, end to end, matching the repo record | 20 | SO2 |
| Judgment: explains why this approach and what credible alternatives were rejected | 20 | SO1 |
| Technical depth: answers "what breaks if X" with correct, specific reasoning about the system's behavior and limits | 20 | SO6 |
| Role and team accountability: articulates what their named role (PM, AI Coordinator, Quality Owner) required this term and how the team's decisions actually got made | 20 | SO5 |
| Communication: clear, honest, right level of detail for the audience, handles not-knowing gracefully | 20 | SO3 |

"Handles not-knowing gracefully" is real: "I don't know, but here is how I would find out" scores; bluffing does not.

:::note[NDA teams]
The defense is identical on Track A and Track B: everything happens live and no artifact changes hands, so NDA constraints do not apply.
:::
