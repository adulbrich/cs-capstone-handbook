Once per term, between week 8 and finals week, your team meets a TA or instructor who is not your team's regular TA for a defense session separate from your [TA check-ins](/assignments/introduction/#ta-check-ins), during which each student walks through something they own and answers live questions. The assessor contacts your team to schedule it, and it can run over a video call with every student's camera on. Your own TA does not attend, so the account you give of your work has to stand on its own.

Bring two or three things you authored or drove this term, the more recent the better, such as a PR, a subsystem, an experiment or analysis, an ADR, or a test or deployment strategy; teammates may bring the same shared work as long as each owns a part of it, and the assessor picks a different artifact or part for each student. Prepare each the way a behavioral interview question asks for it: the situation, what you did and what you rejected, what you handed to AI tools, the result, and what you would do differently now. Each criterion in the rubric opens with the question the assessor asks.

Whether you delegate to AI tools is your call, and neither choice costs anything by itself; the [AI policy](/assignments/introduction/#ai-policy-applies-to-every-assignment) says what is assessed. Say what you handed to AI tools, if anything, and how it went, including what went wrong; naming a failure and the check it lacked never lowers your score.

## How the Session Runs

The session is ten minutes per student plus about ten minutes for setup and wrap, so a team of three runs about forty minutes and a team of five about an hour.

**Ground rules:** your docs, your repo, and your AI tools are open for finding things (search, navigation, opening the file you mean) and closed for answering (no asking a model to explain the code or produce the answer live). Notes are fine. Slides are unnecessary.

The speaking order is drawn at random at the start of the session, never published in advance, and each student gets a different "what breaks if X" question and a sprint from this term, picked at random, to account for.

Your teammates are the people who know whether your account of your own work is true, so claim only what they would co-sign.

If a score comes out borderline, or if your part of the session ran short for any reason, your assessor may ask you back for a brief individual follow-up. It is routine and it is not a penalty.

:::note[If your project is under NDA]
Show your work on a shared screen, within what your partner allows, with partner-confidential specifics [described in neutral terms](/introduction/ip-nda-and-licensing/#teams-under-an-nda); what you show stands in for any record the assessor cannot open. If your partner allows nothing on screen, contact the instruction team as soon as you know.
:::

## What You Submit

Nothing. The defense is live.

## Rubric

TAs and instructors score each student live on a printed sheet (the [example sheet](/defense-live-sheet-example.pdf) shows exactly what is scored).

Your [team charter](/assignments/team-charter/#1-roles) names your role or ownership area for the role criterion, and its chairing rotation; chairing means driving a decision to a conclusion. If you hold two of the three roles (Project Manager, AI Coordinator, Quality Owner), speak to both.

When your communication answer is about work you reviewed, the record is your reviewer notes in the [sprint notes](/assignments/sprint-notes/#what-got-done-and-who) or the review comments themselves, on a PR, an ADR, or an RFC.
