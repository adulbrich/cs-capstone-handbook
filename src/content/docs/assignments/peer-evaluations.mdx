Peer evaluations are how you evaluate the contributions of your teammates, and they evaluate yours. The midterm survey is a pulse check and an early-warning channel; the end-of-term survey carries real weight.

Both surveys are scored per student from what your teammates answer. The midterm survey every term and the end-of-term survey in fall and winter use [four criteria and a 100-point distribution](#the-per-term-instrument); the end-of-term survey in spring is the [CATME survey](#the-catme-survey), with a rubric of its own.

:::caution
Not completing a peer evaluation scores 50% on that survey's share of the grade for the student who did not complete it.
:::

All answers are anonymous; results are shared with instructors only. Questions about grades go to the instructors directly.

## The Per-Term Instrument

In every midterm survey, and in the end-of-term survey in fall and winter, you assess yourself and your teammates on four criteria, then allocate 100 points across all team members including yourself, and finally give qualitative feedback. The instrument, its scale, and the scoring below follow Gardner et al. [2].

### Quantitative Questions

The **first task** is to rate each member of the team, including yourself, one page per member, on these criteria:

Each rating uses a 1 to 5 scale, the same for every criterion:

<PeerCriteria csv={rubricCsv} sourceLabel="canvas/assignments/peer-evaluation/peer-evaluation-rubric.csv" part="scale" />

The numbers only compare across teams if everyone means the same thing by them. This is what each rating looks like; rate the term, not the last week.

| Criterion | A 5 looks like | A 3 looks like | A 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity | Took a fair share every sprint and picked up slack when someone was blocked; the contribution line in every [sprint note](/assignments/sprint-notes/) names work they owned. | Did what was assigned, on time, most sprints; nothing beyond it. | Contribution lines empty, or written by a teammate on their behalf, as a pattern. |
| Quality | Their work is accepted after few review rounds, code arrives with tests, and nothing is reopened as a bug. | Work passes review after the normal back-and-forth. | Work is redone by others, or ships without tests and breaks things. |
| Attitude as a team player | At every meeting they did not excuse in advance, on time; answers messages within one working day; volunteers for unowned work; says early when blocked. | Attends, responds within two working days, does what is asked when asked. | Misses meetings without notice, goes quiet for days, and teammates have stopped assigning them work. |
| Technical value | Owns a part of the system others depend on, and the team asks them when it breaks. | Contributes working pieces that fit; nothing of theirs is called or imported by others' work. | No technical contribution the team can point to. |

Check before you rate. Open the sprint notes and look for the contribution line each sprint; open the repository and the team's tracker and count the work each person owned, reviews given, and issues closed; think back over who was at the meetings. A rating you cannot connect to something you saw is a guess, and the instructor reads the comments against the same record (NDA teams: at the walkthrough or the defense).

For the **second task**, you distribute 100 points among team members, including yourself. Each member can receive between 0 and 100, and the survey does not submit until the total is 100. Give yourself at least 100/N points, rounded down, where N is the number of team members; the survey shows your minimum. A self share below the minimum is raised to it, and your teammates' shares are scaled down so the total stays 100. A team of two has no minimum and its own review rule (see [Teams of Two](#teams-of-two)).

### Qualitative Feedback

You're also asked for comments, which help the instructors understand team dynamics:

- On each member's page, anything about that member's contribution the instructors should know.
- For particularly high or low allocations, provide concrete examples. Which behaviors were particularly valuable or detrimental?
- Overall, how effectively is your team working? Explain.
- To close the midterm survey: Propose one concrete team experiment or activity to try in the next sprint.
- To close the end-of-term survey in fall and winter: What did you learn about working in a team that you will carry into the next term?

## Team Sizes

The survey and the score work for any team of up to 10 members, yourself included ([For Students](/introduction/for-students/#how-you-get-your-project-and-team) has how teams are formed). The score calculation below is normalized around a team of five and explicitly corrects for team size; it is applied identically to every team size.

## Grade Calculation

For the **four criteria**:

1. Convert ratings to a 50-100 scale: 1 = 50, 2 = 62.5, 3 = 75, 4 = 87.5, 5 = 100.
2. Average the ratings each member received, excluding self-ratings.

For the **100-point distribution**:

1. Verify that each rater's total is 100, which the survey enforces, and raise a self-allocation below 100/N points, rounded down, to that floor, scaling the rater's other allocations so the total stays 100 (a team of two has no floor; see [Teams of Two](#teams-of-two)).
2. Average the points each member received, excluding self-allocation.
3. Normalize for team size: multiply the average by N, divide by 5.
4. Clamp the result to [10, 40].
5. Apply <Latex formula="0.65 + 0.0225\cdot x - 0.00025\cdot x^2"/>, where <Latex formula="x"/> is the clamped average.

The formula's output is a multiplier bounded between 0.85 and 1.15; multiply it by 100 to get the distribution score (85 to 115). Scoring over 100 means teammates rated your contribution above an equal share, and you are rewarded for it.

The final peer score is the average of five numbers: the four criteria scores and the distribution score.

Worked example, team of 4: teammates' ratings average to 4, 4, 5, 4 on the four criteria, converting to 87.5, 87.5, 100, 87.5. On the distribution, teammates gave the student an average of 28 points; normalized for team size (× 4 ÷ 5) that is 22.4, within the [10, 40] clamp; the formula gives 0.65 + 0.0225(22.4) − 0.00025(22.4²) = 1.03, so the distribution score is 103. Final peer score: (87.5 + 87.5 + 100 + 87.5 + 103) / 5 = **93.1**.

## Teams of Two

On a team of two, every score you receive comes from your one teammate. On the four-criteria survey, there is no self-allocation floor: an even split is 50 points each, and an instructor reviews a split outside 45 to 55. On either survey, an instructor reviews a two-person team against the repo record and partner feedback before the score stands when the ratings you received average below 3 on the 1 to 5 scale, or when your average rating of your teammate and theirs of you are 1.5 points or more apart. A single teammate's ratings never finalize a low score on a team of two without corroboration.

## The CATME Survey

The end-of-term survey in spring asks about the [CATME Five Teamwork Dimensions](https://info.catme.org/features/catme-five-dimensions/) [1] instead of the four criteria, and has no 100-point distribution. You rate yourself and each teammate on five dimensions: contributing to the team's work, interacting with teammates, keeping the team on track, expecting quality, and having relevant knowledge, skills, and abilities.

Each dimension uses a 1 to 5 scale with a description of the behavior at 5, 3, and 1, and 4 and 2 sit between them. The descriptions are in the [rubric](#end-of-term-survey-catme); read them before you rate, and connect each rating to something in the record: the sprint notes, the repository, the tracker, and the meetings. The survey also asks for a comment on each teammate and on how the team is working.

The score comes from the ratings alone:

1. For each dimension, average the ratings you received, excluding your self-rating.
2. Convert each average to the 50-100 scale: 1 = 50, 2 = 62.5, 3 = 75, 4 = 87.5, 5 = 100.
3. Your score is the mean of the five converted averages. With no distribution, the maximum is 100.

The completion penalty and the review on a team of two apply as they do to every other survey.

## What You Submit

Nothing to upload: you answer each [Qualtrics](https://www.qualtrics.com/) survey from the link sent by email, before it closes; it is not reopened.
